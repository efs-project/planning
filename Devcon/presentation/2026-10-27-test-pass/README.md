## What changed

- Sixteen teaching slides now have a consistent TEST or CHECK band in the
  template's navy and amber. Each connects a concept to an action the audience
  can try on its own app. Title, vision, transition and closing pages stay clear.
- Slide 11 checks whether a complete client starts. Slide 12 separately checks
  whether the user can complete an authorized task without company login.
  SIWE and OAuth are explained without treating either as a complete solution.
- Slides 15 and 16 share a stable example record, author, note and attachment.
  Editable notes-editor and social-feed mockups make two uses of the same
  work recognizable. The attachment revision is explicitly named.
- Slide 17 gives IPFS, Arweave and user-held backups as options for retained
  copies. It does not prescribe a storage stack or claim deployed redundancy.
- Slide 18 uses the original EFS logo and keeps the split between onchain file
  details, offchain copies and client-side fingerprint checks.
- Slide 19 uses wallet balance and transaction-history views to distinguish
  node/RPC access from indexing. Helios and The Graph stay bounded examples.
- Slide 24 adds a network-recovery rehearsal. Slide 25 asks whether an outsider
  can use public instructions and complete the task without contacting the team.
- Slide 27 recaps Interface, Data and Rules with plainer actions.
- Slide 28 replaces the index rebuild with an illustrative import exercise.
  A saved note imports but its attachment is missing. During practice, reconnect
  the original system, save all required files, then block it again and retry.
  Success means the note and attachment are usable in another app.
- "Walk away" and "full stack" have no hyphens in the active slides and notes.
  Source URLs, historical snapshots and the accepted portal proposal are unchanged.
- Spacing, contrast, diagram fit and closing readability were polished. Notes
  keep spoken explanation first, optional qualifications next and sources last.

The human stakes, positive CROPS/hyperstructure vision, three chapters and
30-slide order are preserved. EFS remains a relevant example, not a product
tour. No required live demo, new engineering project or organizer submission
was added. The mockups and import sequence are illustrations, not screenshots
or performed runtime tests.
