# J3 — adoption-first judgement: who uses EFS 2.0 in year one, and why

**Judge:** J3-adoption-first (product and adoption reviewer) · **Date:** 2026-09-02 · **Vault:** the planning vault read-only
**Inputs:** all 22 Phase 1 reader maps (R1–R20), the efsv2 spine (`README.md`, `system-constitution.md`, `core-architecture-candidate.md`, `owner-rulings.md`, `owner-decision-inbox.md`), the web-client-os spine (`README.md`, `mvp-and-acceptance.md`, `product-constitution-and-roadmap.md` §Feature horizons/§Product success measures), `Reviews/2026-08-13-efs2-stage-a-corpus/STATUS.md`, `Reviews/2026-08-13-claude-evidence-round/README.md`, `Open-Decisions.md`, `Milestones.md`, `Kanban.md` (grep), `Decisions.md` (2026-07-29 → 2026-08-13 entries), and the five synthesis docs of `Reviews/2026-07-29-target-communities/` (`README.md`, `opportunity-map.md`, `mainstream-candidate-ranking.md`, `requirements-and-first-apps.md`, `shortlist-red-team.md`).
**Lens:** I ask one question of every design: *which real person or community will use this in the first year, and what would make them choose it over a signed manifest plus a static site?* I do not re-litigate Core correctness (J1/J2 and the seam hunters do that); I cite their findings where adoption depends on them.

---

## 0. Headline

The vault knows how to find its first users and has not used that knowledge. James commissioned target-community research on 2026-07-29 ("find EFS's most promising *first* communities — real populations, not abstract use cases … **who has already lost an archive**", `Decisions.md` line 31); it landed 2026-08-07 with a ranked shortlist, a six-gate go/no-go rule, a conventional-baseline challenge, and a named next action (a 10-day steward-proof sprint). Since then the project has chosen an MVP (direction 2, 2026-08-14), written ~600 KB of client/OS/App-Store/Media design, run a Stage A Core pass, and run an evidence round that falsified the Arcade thesis for its catalog — and **no document on the MVP path cites the community research, no steward has been contacted, the sprint has no card, and the MVP names no user.** The only set that consumed the research is Media Library, which defers its own first product to V2-F2.

The direction is mostly right: greenfield reset, standalone Core, direct guest read first, a write-capable File Browser as the contract-debugging harness, standards-first client, honest `UNKNOWN`. It is over-scoped in exactly the way the 2026-07-22 ruling warned against ("The contraction gate comes later … write the short constitution and explicit support matrix, and only then choose the MVP", `Designs/efsv2/owner-rulings.md` §2026-07-22): five product lanes designed in parallel for an imagined complete OS, an acceptance document that mixes three MVP journeys with Service-Worker generations and Wasm runners, and an on-chain obligation bundle nobody has priced since the owner said "PAY for it" on 2026-07-15. The first user of everything currently specified is the project itself. That is a legitimate thing to build — a disposable harness that debugs contracts that do not yet exist — but it must be called that, and the one adoption decision that matters (name the first steward, or say "none until Core is measured") is in no queue.

**Verdict:** `mostly-right-but-overscoped`.
