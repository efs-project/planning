## 1. The backdating guarantee, re-derived and attacked

I re-derived §1.2 independently before reading §1.3–§1.5 in detail, then attacked the joints. The derivation core holds: P1–P4 are each verified doctrine ([[kel#8.2 Admission-time ruling]]; R-D9; [[kel#6. Event state machine]]), Theorem 1's argument (identical input sets ⇒ identical acceptance) is valid, and Theorem 3's (a)/(b) split does correspond to the two-lane kernel ([[kel#8.3 Confluence boundary]]). The mechanics also survive the named races:

**F-5 — NOTE (affirming, with a named precondition).** *Same-block races and reorgs do not break the ordering claim.* A rotation/revocation and a record admission in one block resolve by transaction execution order: admission step 3/5 ([[kel#8.2 Admission-time ruling]]) reads current state, so an earlier-in-block epoch bump deterministically rejects the envelope's stale `authEpoch`, and `admissionOrdinal` gives a per-transaction total order for `w < r`. Reorg at the admission boundary is covered by the finalized-basis rule (receipts are state; pre-finality receipts can vanish; `AuthProof` binds a finalized basis; gates fail closed — R-K9/R-X3, [authority-model §1.7](./authority-model.md)). **However**, the default-deny theorem silently inherits two *open* seams as preconditions: the first-authoritative-admission rule's collision/front-run/promotion vectors ([[human-overview#7. The seams that must be closed]] seam 4) and the receipt/admission non-transplantability binding (seam 5, `receiptId` does not yet commit the full receipt body). §1's derivation should name these as assumptions, not treat them as settled — if seam 5 closes badly, "no primary admission before `r` ⇒ no strong grade" is forgeable by receipt transplant. **Repair:** one sentence in §1.5 citing seams 4/5 as preconditions; no design change.

**F-1 — SERIOUS. Promotion across a revocation boundary is unspecified, and as currently written it permanently strands the honest victim's own pre-theft evidence — while the lane sells promotion as "already-designed."**

- **The claim attacked:** [authority-model §1.4](./authority-model.md) — ordered evidence is "upgradeable via promotion," needing "no new kernel machinery"; §1.3 — "Promotion W2→W3 exists and is exactly once per claim."
- **The failure scenario:** Alice's laptop signs a record and admits it to the **evidence lane** at ordinal 900 (grant live, unchecked — that is the lane's definition). Her phone is stolen; she revokes; the revocation lands at `r = 1000`. At ordinal 1100 she (recovered, new epoch) tries to promote the ordinal-900 record. [[kel#8.1 Required signed seam]]: "a formerly evidence-only claim may be promoted exactly once **after a valid home admission**" — and a valid home admission runs the full grant check against **current** state (steps 3–5 of [[kel#8.2 Admission-time ruling]]), where the grant is revoked and the epoch is gone. Promotion fails. Forever. Yet Theorem 2's own condition is satisfied: the record was witnessed in the same ordering domain at `w = 900 < r = 1000` — the *existence* fact that anti-backdating needs is on-chain. The design false-rejects exactly the honest-victim case that §1.6.1 uses to motivate the strong grade ("the dilemma is the weak grade; the strong grade dissolves it by making witnessing available before revocation" — but witnessing before revocation turns out **not** to be enough under the written promotion rule; only *checked admission* before revocation is).
- **Why this is more than a nit:** the evidence lane is marketed (correctly) as the cheap unconditional on-ramp (R-AU1, D-4 recommended arm) with promotion as the upgrade path ([use-cases](./use-cases.md) G-FILES-1 "make permanent"). If promotion is check-at-promotion-time, then any revocation event between evidence admission and promotion silently converts "witnessed early, promote later" from an upgrade path into a trap, and the correct product advice becomes "promote *immediately* or your witness ordinal buys you nothing authorization-wise." Neither lane states this.
- **Repair options (designer seam, wire into the seam-3/4 coordinated re-cut, not an owner decision):**
  - **(a) Accept and state plainly:** promotion requires live authorization at promotion time; a pre-`r` evidence ordinal refutes *dating claims* (`EVIDENCE-ORDERED@N` keeps that value) but never upgrades authorization. Change §1.4's "upgradeable" wording and the product copy. *How this repair breaks:* the strong grade's coverage of honest work shrinks in exactly the theft scenarios it was built for; readers must be taught that `EVIDENCE-ORDERED@N, N < r` + a later-revoked grant is a strictly-weaker-than-strong but better-than-nothing state — more vocabulary burden on the lens lane.
  - **(b) Retroactive-basis promotion:** allow promotion where the grant check is evaluated **as of the evidence ordinal** `N`. Mechanically possible only because the witness and KEL share one domain (the co-location corollary earning its keep), but the check is no longer an O(1) current-state read — it needs historical grant/epoch state at `N`, i.e. either client-assembled proofs verified boundedly on-chain or state snapshots per epoch. *How this repair breaks:* new kernel surface and gas; a bounded-verification design does not exist; it partially recreates Theorem 3(b)'s "readers re-derive" inside the kernel; and it must not let a thief evidence-admit early and legitimize later (the thief's pre-theft-window evidence admissions would also become promotable — which is *consistent* with the pre-revocation-window honesty in §1.7, but widens that window's effective reach).
- Recommendation: (a) now, (b) priced as an E-class candidate only if a real journey demands it. Either way the lane text overclaims today.

**F-2 — SERIOUS. Censorship is priced as pure liveness; selective censorship of the *revocation itself* is an integrity-adjacent attack that mints genuine receipts for the thief.**

- **The claim attacked:** [authority-model §1.7](./authority-model.md) "the grade inherits its domain's integrity, not its liveness. A censoring realm cannot forge receipts, but it can refuse admission — starving a principal of the strong grade (not of evidence)"; §5.5 same framing.
- **The failure scenario:** the thief steals Alice's phone key and colludes with (or is) the realm's sequencer. Alice submits the grant revocation / `authEpoch` bump. The sequencer delays *that one transaction* while continuing to admit the thief's record flood under the still-live grant. Every thief record gets a **genuine** `AuthReceipt` — the grant really was unrevoked at each admission ordinal. Integrity holds in the narrow sense; the security story ("revoke fast to bound the window," §1.7's "mitigations are operational") is gutted, because the pre-revocation window's length is now **adversary-controlled up to the venue's force-inclusion latency**. This is materially different from the named attack (refusing a principal's admissions), and neither lane names it.
- **Repair:** reprice §1.7/§5.5: the pre-revocation window is bounded below by theft-to-detection and above by *revocation-inclusion latency under adversarial ordering*; therefore force-inclusion / direct-submission latency is a **security parameter of the strong grade**, not merely a liveness metric — add it as an explicit rider on E1 ([[owner-decision-inbox#Decide after evidence — do not answer yet|E1]] already lists "force inclusion" but as a venue-quality axis, not as the strong grade's window bound). Fixed grant expiries ([[kel#7.3 Revocation rules]]) remain the backstop and should be motivated by exactly this attack. *How the repair breaks:* on venues without credible force inclusion (many L3s), the honest statement becomes "the strong grade's anti-backdating holds, but its window is operator-bounded" — which feeds the T1 venue-class rider ([authority-model §2.6](./authority-model.md)) and may make some venue classes unacceptable authority homes regardless of data-persistence guarantees. That is a feature of honesty, not a bug.

**F-3 — NOTE. Mempool front-running of the revocation is the sharp instance of the window and deserves naming.** Even on an honest-sequencer venue, the revocation transaction is visible before inclusion; a thief watching the mempool gets one last admitted flood ahead of `r`, all with genuine receipts and arbitrarily backdated `claimedAt`/`order` testimony. §1.7 technically covers this ("between theft and revocation") but the product implication — *the act of revoking alerts the thief; private submission lanes matter* — is unstated. Readers comparing `claimedAt` against `admissionOrdinal` see the anomaly only if the lens surfaces both; that is already R-AU6's tuple, so the repair is one line of emphasis, not machinery.

**F-4 — NOTE (affirming). The witness-ladder completeness claim (§1.3, flagged PLAUSIBLE and "the strongest claim in §1" by the lane itself) survived three construction attempts.** (i) *Freshness beacons* (embedding a recent blockhash) prove created-*after*, and created-before is what anti-backdating needs; old blockhashes are public, so a thief embeds last year's beacon trivially — reduces to nothing. (ii) *VDF/proof-of-elapsed-time timestamping* is an existence witness in a compute-bound clock with no verifiable embedding into `D_K` — reduces to W1. (iii) *Quorum-of-foreign-witnesses* (N chains + attestors) is W1 with more signatures; no embedding improves. I could not exhibit a witness service outside W0–W3. Upgrade the lane's confidence on ladder completeness from PLAUSIBLE toward attacked-and-standing — with the F-10 caveat that W1's "no embedding" row is wrong for one realm-pair class.

**F-16 — NOTE (affirming). The weak grade is priced honestly.** §1.6's five cannot-promises each trace to a theorem; the "can promise, honestly and forever" list contains no overclaim I could construct a counterexample to; the false-accept/false-reject dilemma is stated as the weak grade rather than hidden. The charge's "does the lane overclaim what portable evidence shows?" — no. The one adjacent overclaim found is F-1's "upgradeable," which sits on the intermediate, not the floor.

## 2. The L1 pointer, both branches attacked

**F-6 — SERIOUS. The refutation branch rests on a factual error: a genesis-committed home is *verifiable*, not *discoverable*. "A reader of the principal word knows the realm" is false.**

- **The claim attacked:** [authority-model §2.4](./authority-model.md) "Under genesis-committed homes: nothing [breaks] for discovery (it is inside the ID; a reader of the principal word knows the realm)"; echoed in §2.5.3 ("discovery inside the ID") and [aa-inversion §6.2.2](./aa-inversion.md) ("the principal *names* its home; discovery is free").
- **Why it is wrong:** `identityWord = keccak256(DOMAIN_IDENTITY_V2, canonical(GenesisBodyV1))` ([[kel#4.4 Born-KEL identity]]). Keccak is one-way. `authorityHomeRef` is *inside the preimage*; a reader holding only the 32-byte principal can **verify** a claimed home (given the genesis body) but cannot **extract** it. Discovery under genesis-committed homes therefore requires an out-of-band channel carrying the preimage or a home hint: the home itself (circular for bootstrap), a replicated genesis record on some venue the reader already watches, a social/hint row (§2.5.4 — which becomes *safe* here, because any hint is checkable against the digest, unlike the forgeable movable-home hint), or realm enumeration.
- **Failure scenario:** a merged multi-realm client (pass ruling 2's "future unified view"; R-XC1's hard case) meets a bare `bytes32` principal cited in a record on realm X. Under the lane's text it "knows the realm." In fact it knows nothing: if no hint/preimage is reachable, "which realm speaks for P" is unanswerable — precisely the discovery cost the lane's own point 2 (§0) says is "the entire content of the pointer question."
- **What survives, what changes:** the *security* analysis survives fully — unforgeability (a thief cannot re-home a digest principal) is a preimage property, and hint-plus-digest-check is unforgeable discovery. What changes is the **pricing of Decision A5**: arm C (write-once discovery pointer) was priced as "freeze bytes for a NICE"; its actual value is exactly the un-hinted bootstrap and merged-view case that genesis commitment does *not* cover. A5a likely still wins (hints + preimage replication are cheap, permissionless, and verifiable — an ordinary Durable convention), but that must now be *argued*, and the §2.4 sentence corrected. *How the hint-convention repair breaks:* hint availability is exactly the class of best-effort replication the mission refuses to promise; a principal whose hints all rot is undiscoverable-but-verifiable — honest, but it must be said in A5's trail.

**F-7 — SERIOUS. The justified branch contains an internal contradiction: the pointer as designed cannot deliver the very scenario the lane's own text presents as the strongest argument for it.**

- **The claims in tension:** [authority-model §5.5](./authority-model.md): censorship of a principal "is the strongest honest argument *for* the re-home promise — it belongs in Decision A5's trail." [authority-model §2.1](./authority-model.md): "*Retarget (re-home)* requires a proof that the **source home authorized departure** — a finalized `MIGRATE_PREPARE`-class KEL event **at the current home**." A5's example ("years later X's sequencer censors her") invites James to weigh re-home as the answer to censorship.
- **The failure scenario:** realm X's sequencer censors Alice. Alice initiates re-home to Y. Step one is admitting `MIGRATE_PREPARE` at X — the realm that is censoring her. The censor censors the departure event exactly as it censors everything else. The two-phase design (correctly, for no-dual-admission) delegates pointer-update authority to the home being left; against a *cooperative* home this is the right anti-theft rule (§2.3's theorem), and against an *adversarial* home it is a dead end. [[kel#4.5 Home topology: canonical locator plus co-located authority]] already admits the sibling case: "V1 does not promise same-principal rescue from a home that dies before a finalized prepare; that needs a separately reviewed L1 emergency-recovery verifier." Chains-don't-die makes literal home-death out of scope, but a censoring home is *precisely equivalent* for this purpose: readable state, unadmittable departure.
- **Consequence:** A5b as written buys **voluntary venue switching only**. Censorship escape — the motivating story — requires an additional L1-side escape hatch that verifies KEL recovery-class authority *without* source cooperation (an emergency verifier proving "the committed recovery/veto quorum signs the departure" directly on L1, with a long delay). That is a substantially bigger and currently unsolved surface (it re-opens the registry-as-authority-root worry §2.1 explicitly avoids, since L1 would now adjudicate a departure the home never admitted). A5b's option text must disclose this or James decides on a false bill. Escalated as Decision RT-1 below.
- *How the escape-hatch repair breaks:* an L1 emergency departure path is a new thief target — a thief who wins the recovery quorum (already "wins everything," §5.2) now also gets a censor-bypassing exit lane; and it weakens §2.1's clean "the registry never becomes an independent authority root," because in the emergency path it does. The delay/veto machinery must be at least as strong as recovery's, and the co-location corollary means records admitted at X during the contested departure need explicit grading. None of this is designed.

**F-8 — NOTE.** The retarget delay window's veto is an L1 transaction; the design inherits L1 inclusion liveness during `eligibleAtBlock` (same family as F-2, much weaker in practice given L1 force-inclusion properties). Name it in the A5b bill; no design change.

**F-9 — NOTE.** Under A5b every anchored principal touches L1 once — a de facto global registration layer. The lane correctly shows it is not an *authority* hub (update authority delegated to homes), but it is a *discovery* hub with fee/censorship concentration at inception time, which is the T2 stop-rule's other half. §2.6 T2's reconciliation is honest about merits but should say this concentration cost out loud rather than only "L1 registration economics."

**Squatting/poisoning (§2.2): attacked, holds.** Born-KEL entries are unforgeable-by-construction (registering P requires P's genesis preimage; early third-party submission of the *correct* entry is harmless). Bare principals: the symmetric-race honesty (`LEGACY-HOME-CONTESTED`) is correct and correctly labeled; no registry design can fix symmetric key knowledge. The pointer-theft theorem (§2.3 — "the pointer can never be stronger than the recovery machinery of the identity it points for") survives attack and is the strongest new result in the lane; F-7 is its unstated dual (*the pointer can never be stronger than the source home's willingness to admit the departure*). State both.

## 3. N1 decomposition audit

Method applied as the charge demands: for pairs of axes, attempt to construct a scenario where answering one forecloses another; check each axis framing for smuggled recommendations; test the FS-vs-OS axis's separability.

**Independence pair tests (constructed, mostly passed).** AX-1=No collapses AX-2/AX-3 — but the lane states this conditionality openly ("root of the decision tree"), which is legitimate tree structure, not a false independence claim. AX-2(a) moots AX-4's cross-realm case "until a second realm exists" — admitted in AX-2's coupling paragraph. AX-5(a)+AX-2(b) (one required conformance profile + permissionless realms at launch) is coherent (all realms run the one profile). AX-3(a) stays coherent under every AX-2 arm. The §3.8 contradiction rows were each re-checked and are genuine (movable-no-locator really does violate R-K11 via §2.3 row 3; AX-1(b)+AX-3(b) really is vacuous). **The decomposition is genuinely answerable axis-by-axis**, with the two qualifications below.

**F-10 — SERIOUS. The co-location corollary — the load-bearing beam under AX-2, AX-4, and the grade table — overstates domain incomparability for the dominant real-world realm-pair class: rollups sharing a settlement layer.**

- **The claim attacked:** [authority-model §1.2](./authority-model.md) corollary — "Independent chains do not form a total order — Solana slots and Ethereum blocks have no verifiable mutual embedding"; consequence 3 — "Cross-domain 'witnessed on X before revoked on Y' is at best a timestamp heuristic and must never earn the strong grade"; W1's row — "strict order against the KEL domain (no embedding)."
- **The counterexample:** let X and Y both be rollups settling on Ethereum L1. Both post state commitments to L1 in L1 transactions; L1 gives them a shared, *cryptographically verifiable*, coarse ordering domain: "X's batch containing admission `w` was finalized to L1 at L1-block `b_w`; Y's batch containing revocation `r` was finalized at `b_r`; `b_w < b_r`" is a **provable** cross-realm order statement — not a timestamp heuristic. Granularity is batch-level (minutes-to-hours for validity rollups; the optimistic dispute window complicates *when* the commitment is trustworthy but not the eventual order); it is a partial embedding, sound in one direction (finalized-before implies existed-before), which is exactly the direction Theorem 2 needs for existence bounds.
- **What it does and does not break:** it does **not** break the two-grade theorem — the authorization check still ran nowhere but the home realm; a settlement-anchored bound is an existence/freshness fact, mappable to the lane's own orthogonal axes. It **does** break: (i) the corollary's universality — the "no embedding" premise is realm-pair-class-dependent, and the lane's citation ([[solana#6.6 Authority and sovereignty]]) covers the sovereign-pair case only; (ii) W1's table row, which needs a W1.5-class entry (foreign domain **with** verifiable partial embedding); (iii) AX-4's pricing — "a shared social identity … participates in FS realm X at snapshot/evidence grade" with *policy-chosen* freshness understates the shared-settlement case, where the staleness bound of an `AuthProof@H` is **verifiable against L1 order** rather than trusted per policy. Since James's frame is "chains render like drives" over EVM chains — in practice mostly rollups sharing L1 settlement — this is the common case, not a corner.
- **Failure scenario for leaving it unrepaired:** the A4 packet tells James cross-realm participation is capped at policy-trusted snapshot grade; he prices realm-local deployment (A2/A4) on that basis; a later designer correctly implements verifiable settlement-anchored bounds; the owner decision was made on an understated floor.
- **Repair:** qualify the corollary per realm-pair class (sovereign pairs: heuristic only, as written; shared-settlement pairs: verifiable coarse embedding, existence bounds provable, authorization still home-only). Add the settlement-anchored bound to the read vocabulary as a freshness/existence form (it is *not* a new authorization grade — the two-grade result survives, and saying so explicitly is the cleanest proof the taxonomy is doing real work). Re-check A2/A4 option text. *How the repair breaks:* optimistic-rollup dispute windows make "finalized to L1" itself a graded statement (the embedding is only as good as the fraud-proof assumption during the window); the vocabulary gains another axis value and the lens lane inherits rendering it honestly; and none of it is measurement-backed (E1 rider).

**F-11 — NOTE. One quiet foreclosure in AX-2's framing.** "Adopt realm-qualified authority as an invariant now" is presented as the rule *every* option obeys, so no A2 arm offers unqualified global `CURRENT`. At one shipped realm the difference is vacuous, and R-K11's logic is strong — but the decomposition method promised axes where "different answers produce different frozen surface," and here the framing itself adopts a semantic stance (there is no privileged realm whose answers need no qualifier) before James answers. Honest fix: one line in A2 stating "all arms adopt the invariant; the rejected alternative (unqualified CURRENT from one privileged venue) is foreclosed by R-K11's dual-claim incoherence, not offered" — which is almost certainly correct, but the packet should say it is foreclosing, not hide it. No smuggled recommendation found in AX-1/AX-3/AX-5 framings; AX-6's refusal to recommend without E1 is exemplary.

**FS-vs-OS separability (AX-4):** the technical dissolution via the corollary is real and elegant for sovereign realm pairs; F-10 partially reopens it for shared-settlement pairs (the "remaining owner question is product-acceptance" conclusion survives, but the acceptance question is now easier than the lane says, because the cross-realm floor is stronger). Ledger item 18's "largely dissolved technically" should carry that qualifier.

## Decisions for James

Only what this attack genuinely escalates. Both are amendments to decisions the lanes already tabled, not new axes.

### RT-1 — Amend A5 (re-home / pointer): split "voluntary move" from "censorship escape" before choosing

**Plain example:** Alice anchored her authority on L2 X. Case 1: X gets expensive and she *wants* to move — the designed pointer (A5b) handles this: X admits her departure event, the L1 delay runs, she re-homes. Case 2: X's sequencer *censors her* — the designed pointer does nothing, because step one is X admitting the departure event she is being censored out of. The lane's own §5.5 presents Case 2 as the strongest argument for the re-home promise, while its §2.1 design only delivers Case 1 (F-7). If you choose A5b believing it answers censorship, you are buying something that is not in the box.

- **Option 1 — A5a unchanged (no re-home promise), with the corrected trail. Recommended.** Censorship is answered as §5.5's other path already says: evidence lane elsewhere + exports + successor identity; the F-7 gap then costs nothing. Also adopt F-6's correction: genesis commitment gives unforgeable home *verification*, not location — discovery bootstrap is a hint/preimage-replication Durable convention, and A5c's write-once pointer is the fallback if that convention is judged too weak for merged multi-realm views.
- **Option 2 — A5b as designed, honestly re-labeled "voluntary re-home only."** Keep the two-phase source-authorized pointer; state in product copy that a censoring or dead-service home is escaped by successor identity, not re-home.
- **Option 3 — A5b plus an L1 emergency-departure verifier (the full censorship-escape promise).** The true bill: everything in A5b *plus* an unsolved L1 verifier that accepts recovery/veto-quorum-signed departures without source cooperation ([[kel#4.5 Home topology: canonical locator plus co-located authority]] names this a freeze blocker), a new thief target (recovery-quorum theft now includes a censor-bypass exit), and a partial breach of "the registry is never an independent authority root."

**Rider (carried from both lanes, extended by F-2):** the T1 venue-class question on the A5 rider should add *revocation force-inclusion latency under adversarial sequencing* as a qualifying property of an acceptable authority home — it bounds the strong grade's theft window, so it is a security parameter, not a convenience metric. Reason trail: F-2, F-7 above; [authority-model §2.1/§2.3/§5.5](./authority-model.md); [aa-inversion §6.2](./aa-inversion.md); [[kel#4.5 Home topology: canonical locator plus co-located authority]]; [use-cases J4](./use-cases.md); [[owner-decision-inbox#Decide after evidence — do not answer yet|E1]].

### RT-2 — Rider on A2/A4: acknowledge the shared-settlement floor before answering

**Plain example:** Alice's identity homes on rollup X; a community filesystem lives on rollup Y; both settle on Ethereum L1. Reason trail: F-10 above; [authority-model §1.2 corollary / §3 AX-4](./authority-model.md); [[solana#6.6 Authority and sovereignty]] (sovereign-pair evidence only); [[owner-rulings]] 2026-07-23 (FS-vs-OS hypothesis).
