## 0. Verdict

**The pass held on architecture and broke on artifacts.** Every lane consumed the typed compiled-policy model without extending its grammar; the taxonomy, the profile family, the 6+1 axis result model, the guest ladder, and the link-safety floor are real deliverables that the replacement spec can lift. But the pass shipped **two mutually incompatible contract-tier objects under one name**, and the two red teams between them found **two false theorems** (`CH-1`; "both execution plans have identical semantics") and **one false claim of structural closure** (GATE isolation) that were already written as conformance gates. A false gate is worse than no gate: it retires the vectors that would have caught the real thing.

Counts: **63 findings adjudicated** (24 AO + 9 AO-C + 39 AV, minus overlaps) plus **9 critic-added findings (CR-\*)**. Of the eight FATALs, seven are CONFIRMED and one (AV-11) is **confirmed in substance, rejected as derived** — its worked example fails on the review's own applicability rule, and the corrected mechanism needs a different repair than the one proposed.

Three rulings do most of the work, and each closes several findings at once:

- **LR-1 (encoding).** One contract-tier artifact: a **packed, fixed-width, offset-free `PlanV1`**, stored as **immutable code at a content-derived address** and read by `EXTCODECOPY`. No contract parses CBOR; no contract trusts a registry. Closes AO-1, AO-3, most of AO-11, AO-24, AO-C1/C4/C5/C9.
- **LR-2 (authority status is claim-conditional).** Principal authority status is evaluated **only for principals holding a PRESENT claim at the position, and only at or above the selected winner.** Closes AO-4's semantic divergence, AO-9's hostage lever, and narrows on-chain UNKNOWN to a three-item list.
- **LR-3 (verify above the winner).** Every plan — direct or roster, on-chain or client — **direct-probes every entry ranked strictly above the selected winner.** The claimant roster becomes a **pure planning hint whose completeness is not a correctness dependency**; the challenge-window recheck becomes decision-scoped; the cached-gate predicate becomes soundable. Closes AO-4's absence laundering, AO-6, and half of AO-5.

The single most consequential *product* finding is **AV-19**: GATE isolation isolates the policy and not the selection, so TUF's rollback attack executes entirely through the presentation layer against a defence the pass calls structural. The single most consequential *safety* finding is **AV-12**: `INCOMPLETE_BUDGET` launders into blessed absence source 3 through snapshot build, producing signed, shareable, durable false absence at the strongest grade in the system.

**Pushback on the rails: none.** Two adopted rulings are stressed and both repairs live inside the design, not in the ruling: item F's challenge-window pattern had no sound instantiation (AO-6 → LR-3), and the four-source absence rule is quietly widened at two places (AV-12, AO-13) — the rule is right, the two sources needed preconditions. One **precedence violation carried in from the settled chapter** is corrected in §2.4 (the `SAME_SLOT_COLLISION` E2 line).

### 1.1 The three unifying rulings (stated once; referenced below)

> **LR-1 — One contract-tier artifact, self-verifying by construction.** *(binding design center; supersedes [core-onchain §2.1–2.3](./core-onchain.md) and reconciles [object-taxonomy ENC-1/2/3](./object-taxonomy.md))*
>
> 1. **`PlanV1` is a packed, big-endian, fixed-width, offset-free byte layout** — the shape of [object-taxonomy §2.2](./object-taxonomy.md)'s `RosterV1`, carrying the header fields [core-onchain §2.1](./core-onchain.md)'s `PlanV1` needs (`semanticsProfileId`, `purposeId`, `scopeId`, `combiner`, `thresholdK`, `relinquishMode`, `entryCount`, plus a bounded advisory-rule section). Total length is an exact function of the entry and advisory counts; all reserved bytes are zero; entries are strictly ascending by `(tier, principal)`; a principal appears at most once. **No contract decodes CBOR, ever.** The deterministic-CBOR profile remains the client-side source/effective form ([object-taxonomy ENC-1](./object-taxonomy.md)).
> 2. **The projection `project(EffectiveLens, sliceKey) → PlanV1 bytes` is normative and byte-exact**, committed by the Effective Lens's `sliceCommitment` (leaf = `keccak256(sliceKey ‖ planId)` — a **whole plan** per leaf, never a sub-plan entry). Three delivery forms, all delivering the **complete** plan: **S** stored, **C** calldata (hashed and compared to the pinned `planId`), **P** whole-plan-plus-Merkle-path to a parent `EffectiveLensId`.
> 3. **Storage is immutable code at a content-derived address.** A plan is deployed as a data-only contract (SSTORE2 shape) at a `CREATE2` address derived from `(pinned deployer, planId, initcode)`; a consumer computes the expected address from `planId` and reads the bytes with `EXTCODECOPY`. **The plan store is therefore not a trusted party**: address derivation *is* the verification. `GateConfig` pins the deployer address (which holds no mutable state) and `planId`; it never pins a mutable registry that answers queries.
> 4. **Naming, fixed:** **Roster** = the ordered principal-entry list (the shared trust-list primitive James's steer names); **Plan** = Roster + combiner + purpose + scope + modes + advisory rules = the executable contract-tier slice and the thing `PlanV1` encodes; **GATE** = the owner-pinned consumption profile that executes a Plan. "Lens" never appears in a contract ABI.
> 5. **Vocabulary, fixed:** the combiner **tag space is global and shared with the client tier** (so an unknown tag is unknown everywhere); the CORE profile **whitelists** `EXACT`, `PRIORITY_FIRST_PRESENT`, `THRESHOLD`, plus advisory point-deny. `UNION_SET`, `ONLY_ONE`, `MERGE`, imports, channels, whiteouts, discovery, and sorting are client-tier and reject at CORE decode.
> 6. **Caps, fixed as two different numbers doing two different jobs:** the **CORE per-plan entry cap** is a profile constant (candidate **64**, benchmark-set) with typed failure past it; the **client compile ceiling** is E6's portable number (candidate 256). Conflating them was the `MAX_LENSES = 20` mistake in a new suit.
> 7. **Deny sources are a plan section, not an entry effect.** `effect = DENY_SOURCE` is deleted from the entry enum; advisory rules carry their own `labelDefinitionId`, source list, action code, `honorStale`, and per-rule bounds.
>
> **Owner:** ENC + CONTRACTS. **Debt:** one Foundry fixture proving decode, address derivation, `EXTCODECOPY` read cost, and the projection round-trip in three languages.

> **LR-2 — Authority status is claim-conditional.** *(binding design center; supersedes [core-onchain §3.4a/§3.5](./core-onchain.md))*
>
> Principal authority status (`IdentityState`, `requireActivePrincipal`, the authorization axis floor) is evaluated **only for principals that hold a PRESENT claim at the resolved position, and only for those ranked at or above the selected winner.** A principal whose slot at the position is empty contributes nothing, and its identity state is never consulted.
>
> Consequences, each independently testable: (i) the direct and roster execution plans return **identical semantics** — the divergence AO-4 found was entirely caused by the over-strict rule; (ii) a listed principal cannot wedge a gate by changing its own KEL status without also claiming at the position (AO-9's lever disappears); (iii) **on-chain `UNKNOWN` is exhaustively**: a PRESENT claim whose authorization axis cannot be graded at the required floor, an unrecognized encoding/profile/suite, or an out-of-profile input; (iv) `requireActivePrincipal` cost falls from `K` to `≤ r`.
>
> This does not weaken anti-fallthrough. Anti-fallthrough forbids treating an *unknown slot state* as absence; on-chain the slot state is never unknown (venue-total), and off-chain an unreadable slot is `UNKNOWN` and still stops. **Owner:** RES + GATE-P + CONTRACTS.

> **LR-3 — Verify above the winner.** *(binding design center; new)*
>
> Every resolution of an exclusive position — direct plan, roster plan, cached revalidation, or challenge-window recheck, on-chain or client — **directly probes the slot of every plan entry ranked strictly above the selected winner** before the winner is accepted. Never fewer.
>
> Consequences: (i) **the claimant roster becomes a pure planning hint** — with the decisive prefix probed directly, a roster omission can cost work but can never produce a wrong answer or a false absence, so roster completeness ceases to be a correctness dependency (this is the honest form of "the roster buys cost, never truth"); (ii) the **challenge-window recheck is decision-scoped** — re-probe the recorded `claimId`'s slot head and revocation state plus the `r` entries above it plus the advisory positions (~5–10 cold words), so unrelated churn at the position no longer vetoes the decision (AO-6); (iii) the cached-gate predicate has a sound prefix component. **Honest cost curve:** the roster's advantage is proportional to the winner's rank being low — the common owner-first case (r = 1–3) keeps the lever; a rank-40 winner pays 40 verification probes and the lever mostly disappears. Say that in the E2 row rather than quoting the low-rank number alone.
>
> **Owner:** RES + GATE-P + CONTRACTS + E2 (the corrected cost curve).

### 1.4 AV findings (profiles, composition, Views, links)

**AV-11 — CONFIRMED IN SUBSTANCE / REJECTED AS DERIVED (and this changes the repair).**

The worked mechanism does not work. AV-11 has Mallory's rule scoped to `/data/2025/**` "applicable" at `/data/2026/results.csv`. Under [review §2.1](../2026-07-11-efsv2-lens-architecture-and-scale-review.md), *applicability* requires the rule's scope to cover the query; a rule scoped to `/data/2025/**` is **not applicable** at `/data/2026/`, so Carol's covering rule supplies the configuration and the position resolves PRESENT. The narrated attack fails on the review's own rule. (VERIFIED by re-derivation.)

The defect is nonetheless real, via a different mechanism: **a *covering* import at higher mount priority with a narrower roster.** Mallory's rule is scoped `/data/**` (applicable everywhere Carol was), wins the class-specific policy key, and its roster omits Carol. At `/data/2026/results.csv` the applicable rule admits only the container owner, who never claimed; Carol's live claim is not admitted by any applicable rule; the result is `ABSENT_PROVEN` with closure over one source, `completeness = FINAL`, and `test -e` returns false at `ENOENT` grade from a strict mount.

So **CH-1's step 3 is false as written** — "composition only adds sources" is wrong, because a higher-priority import *replaces* an applicable rule rather than adding to it — and CH-1 must not be lifted into the replacement spec as a conformance gate in its current form. But the repair AV-11 proposes ("forbid displacement-by-narrowing") is aimed at the mechanism that does not work, and would forbid a legitimate operation (an import that deliberately narrows who may name files in a subtree is a normal, wanted policy).

**Binding repair (four parts):**

1. **CH-1 is restated as relative-to-plan and the relativity is part of the result.** *"No composition can produce `ABSENT_PROVEN` without closure over every source applicable under the compiled plan at the joint basis; the plan's coverage of the position is reported on the result."*
2. **New required result field — rule-coverage provenance.** Every `ABSENT_PROVEN` names the rule (and, per **CR/AV-31**, its authoring origin) under which it was decided, and carries the flag `AMBIENT_BASELINE_ONLY` when no rule of the user's own policy covered the position. A consumer profile may refuse or label ambient-baseline absences; MOUNT-STRICT's acceptance matrix gains a row for it.
3. **Reserved user band.** The user's **directly authored** rules occupy a priority band above **all** imports. No import can displace the user's own tier assignments; imports fill scopes the user left uncovered or sit below. This kills the attack outright for user-authored trust and is a one-line compiler rule over data the compiler already has.
4. **Displacement diff.** When an imported rule takes a class-specific policy key at a scope where the importer already held a rule with a different roster, the ceremony renders it as **"removes N sources at these scopes"** — displacement is a trust change and must read as one, not as a benign coverage note in the exemptions ledger.

**Owner:** COMP + RES + VECT (AV-11's gerrymander fixture, re-cut against the corrected mechanism: a *covering* higher-priority import with a narrower roster; assert the coverage annotation and the reserved-band refusal).

**AV-12 — CONFIRMED (and this is the pass's sharpest safety finding).**

Re-derived: the four-source rule blesses "venue-committed bundle closure manifest" and nothing anywhere states that a manifest may commit only scopes enumerated to `FINAL`. A flood that trips the builder's budget produces `INCOMPLETE_BUDGET(continuation)`; the builder emits a manifest committing the container; every downstream consumer — P-16a's *recommended* ordinary-app mount, offline bundles, `REQUIRE_PROVEN` Views — derives honest-looking `ENOENT` past the cut, at the strongest grade in the system, with a grade-free projection showing nothing. Cost to the attacker: gas for the flood.

**Binding repair (two sentences, both normative):**

1. **A closure manifest may commit only scopes whose enumeration reached completeness `FINAL` at the manifest basis. Budget-incomplete scopes are committed as `PARTIAL(cursor)`, and no consumer may derive absence inside a `PARTIAL` scope.** This is an **extension of FS-LENS/1 §1.7 source 3**, not a reopening: the source stands, it gains a precondition.
2. **A composed result's completeness axis is the minimum over its members** — `INCOMPLETE_BUDGET` dominates `FINAL` — stated beside [profiles-composition §2.6](./profiles-composition.md)'s basis-joint rule, so composition is completeness-joint as well as basis-joint.

**Owner:** the `.efs-bundle` normative spec (joined-pass G-F) + COMP + RES; **VECT:** AV-12's budget→manifest fixture.

**AV-19 — CONFIRMED (the pass's sharpest product finding).**

Re-derived: dataflow isolation keeps URL input out of GATE evaluation, and the human's *selection* is upstream of the gate. A hostile link controls the browse pane's ordering, filtering, and labels (`sort=`/`kind=`/`group=` apply immediately with no offer semantics at all); the user clicks install on the row they were shown; every GATE check passes because the question asked was "may I install *this* artifact?" and the honest answer is yes. GATE/1 rule 6's monotone floors are per-position-already-acted-on, so a first install has no floor. This is TUF's rollback attack executed through the presentation layer against a defence the pass calls structural.

**Binding repair:** the install ceremony **re-derives and displays the candidate set under the GATE's own rules, in System Chrome, at ceremony time, discarding the browse view's ordering** — *"You selected 1.2.0. The publisher's current release is 1.4.1. 1.2.0 is 14 months older and carries 2 advisories."* GATE/1 gains a **current-release resolution obligation**: installing a non-current release is an explicit downgrade confirmation, and the reason (pin, compatibility, deliberate rollback) is recorded in the update-resolution receipt. **Owner:** GATE-P + CLIENT (System Chrome) + VECT.

### 2.3 The kernel-counter ruling (AO-C6, AO-20, AO-21, AO-5, AO-6)

- **`claimantsBySemanticPosition` — the claimant roster — remains a lean-adopt E2 candidate**, but its case must be re-stated with **LR-3's corrected framing** (a pure planning hint whose completeness is not a correctness dependency) and **AO-11's corrected numbers** (~4–7× at the cold single-point gate case, not ~10×; and the advantage decays with the winner's rank).
- **E2 receives one joint row for all three**, with the write-side amplification, the read-side lever, and the honest cost curve stated together. *"Do not let E2 be asked for three competing kernel counters from three lanes with three cost models"* (AO-8's §8 consequence) is adopted verbatim as an instruction to the costing pass.

## 4. The owner packet

Ten items. Each is independently answerable: **answering one adopts nothing else.** Every item states a plain example, real arms, a recommendation with confidence, and its reason trail. Nothing here re-asks a settled item; §4.11 states which held items become askable.

### LP-1 — Ratify the typed-lens constitution (D-13 / N2c revalidated)

**Example.** Today "lens" means nine things: your file view, your friend list, your app's signer roster, a malware labeler set, a link parameter, and the rule a contract uses to decide who may publish an OS update. Ratifying says: **a lens is a typed, purpose-scoped, compiled policy over evidence** — following a friend can never let them publish an OS update, and accepting a malware label never means accepting the labeler's filenames.
**Options.** (a) **Adopt the typed compiled-policy model**: `EvidenceGraph + BasisVector + EffectiveLens + Context → ResolvedView + ViewReceipt`; "lens" stays the human word; the flat ordered author list survives as an editor projection and one execution slice. (b) Keep the ordered author list as the universal primitive.
**Rec: (a), high confidence.** Every lane in two passes compiled every real consumer inside this grammar without extending it; no lane found a behaviour the grammar could not express. The alternative fails the 50+ case semantically before it fails computationally.
**Also confirms (bundled deliberately, because they are the same ratification):** no universal protocol content/advisory tail; personal instantiated policy is local/encrypted by default; every exclusive authority rule declares fallthrough or stop, with packages/updates/security-config/gates stopping by default.
**Trail:** [human-overview D-13](../../Designs/efsv2/human-overview.md) · [review §18.1](../2026-07-11-efsv2-lens-architecture-and-scale-review.md) items 1–4 · [FS-LENS/1 §1.1](../2026-07-25-joined-fs-pass-corpus/filesystem-core.md) · [profiles-composition §1](./profiles-composition.md) · N2c in [owner-decision-inbox](../../Designs/efsv2/owner-decision-inbox.md).

### LP-2 — The on-chain lens promise, updated (D-9 restated)

**Example.** A contract asks "who is authoritative for this one name?" — that works on-chain today, in bounded gas, at every size we care about. A contract asking "give me this whole 128-entry folder, sorted" **cannot be served**: since Fusaka (2025-12-03) Ethereum caps a transaction at 16,777,216 gas, and a 128-item × 55-principal naive page is ~29.5M. This is no longer a question of taste.
**Options.** (a) **Bounded candidate pages + exact venue-local point resolution + deterministic fixed-basis client materialization is the core promise**; wide sorted directories are a client product; contract consumers of whole directories use sparse-roster plans, a materialized/proven snapshot, or move to the client tier. (b) Declare globally sorted / top-N contract-native pages mandatory and fund a separate ordered-index / materialization / proof design as its own program.
**Rec: (a), high confidence** — and note the change since 2026-07-11: the review said such pages "should not be promised"; the live cap says they **cannot be delivered**, permanently, on any 7825 chain. Freeze the vocabulary accordingly.
**Trail:** [review §8.2/§10/§18.1](../2026-07-11-efsv2-lens-architecture-and-scale-review.md) item 11 · [research §3.1–3.2](./research.md) (cap VERIFIED live) · [core-onchain §6](./core-onchain.md) · [attack-onchain §5](./attack-onchain.md) (my corrections to those numbers; the shape verdict is unchanged).

### LP-3 — Naming ratification

**Example.** Every corpus accident of the form "contracts walk lenses" came from one word doing two jobs. The pass proposes a family so each act has its own word: **Lens** (your read-view) · **View** (a saved, linkable lens + place + presentation) · **Starter Pack** (a published curator View for onboarding) · **Follow** (discovery only) · **Channel** (a subscription pointer) · **Labeler** + **Action Map** (who says what vs what you do about it) · **Roster** (the shared trust-list: an ordered set of principals with tiers) · **Plan** (the executable slice a contract reads) · **GATE** (the install/app-store/contract trust function — deliberately machine-cold).
**Options.** (a) **Adopt the family as listed.** (b) Adopt with named changes. (c) Defer naming to implementation.
**Rec: (a), high confidence.** The two words that matter most are **GATE** (so a social lens can never be mistaken for an install policy) and the **Roster/Plan** split (so "the trust list" and "the executable policy" stop being one word — the pass's own two lanes each used "Roster" for a different object, which is the argument).

### LP-4 — The lens-scale structure (E6 restated against the 15–55 centre)

**Example.** Your realistic policy is ~10–50 trusted people plus 3–5 system principals — **not** "50 keys," because your twelve devices collapse behind one identity. Three different numbers have been conflated: what a normal policy holds (15–55), what a contract-readable plan may hold, and what the compiler will accept before it refuses.
**Options.** (a) **Ratify the structure** (numbers stay evidence-gated to E6): the design centre is 15–55 principals; the **CORE per-plan cap** and the **client compile ceiling** are different constants doing different jobs; per-read budgets are independent of policy size; every limit fails **typed** and nothing anywhere truncates; loading a folder from hundreds of curators is a bespoke curation contract that publishes **one** principal, not base-lens growth; web-of-trust stays out of the base model. (b) Keep one number as "the lens limit" (the `MAX_LENSES` shape) and set it by measurement.
**Rec: (a), high confidence.** One number cannot serve both a gas-bounded contract and a compiler with import headroom, and pretending otherwise is exactly how `MAX_LENSES = 20` shipped with silent truncation.
**Rider (from AV-24, worth a yes/no in passing):** the client counts and shows **effective authority principals across all saved Views**, budgeted against the centre — otherwise a year of one-tap "see it as they curate it" reaches 245 principals with the people-list still reading 45.

### LP-5 — The anonymous/guest viewer's default trust grade

**Example.** Someone clicks an EFS link with no account. Their client can *always* verify who signed what and that the bytes match — a hostile server cannot forge authorship. What it cannot do over an ordinary hosted endpoint is prove that a file **isn't** there, or that a folder listing is complete. So: what does the product promise a first-time visitor by default?
**Options.** (a) **G1 by default** — client-side verification of authorship and bytes over a hosted endpoint, absence **never claimed** ("nothing found via this endpoint (unverified)"), grades ratcheting up in place as proofs arrive, and a *visible correction* if a later proof refutes the first render. Gateway-rendered pages (G0) are permitted only labelled as the operator's word. (b) Require a proof-capable path (own node, light client, or signed closure bundle) for the ordinary guest path — more honest, slower, and dependent on infrastructure that does not exist yet. (c) Accept G0 gateway rendering as an ordinary product surface.
**Rec: (a), medium-high confidence.** It is the only arm where the hyperlink product works on day one *and* nothing is claimed that was not verified. The honest cost, which must be said out loud in product copy: **a guest can never be told "that file does not exist"** — only "not found here, unverified."

### LP-6 — Ratify the GATE profile's hard rules

### LP-7 — Personal-policy privacy default

**Example.** Your file *contents* can be public while **who you trust** stays yours. Your policy names your friends, your communities, your moderation sources, your employer's folders, and your political associations — a better profile of you than the files themselves.
**Options.** (a) **Ratify:** personal policy is local/encrypted by default; publishing is a deliberate ceremony with a disclosure preview; **the routine freshness check runs against your own node or replica by default**, and using a remote endpoint for it is a disclosed non-default mode (because the batched check ships your entire trust roster to that endpoint on every app focus, which is a better cross-session identifier than a cookie); links never carry principal arrays, and trust-adjacent link parameters ride the fragment so they are never sent to a server. (b) Ratify the policy but keep the remote batched check as the default (faster, simpler client, leaks the roster). (c) Defer to the privacy pass.
**Rec: (a), high confidence**, with one honest limit stated in the same breath: **a content-addressed public system cannot prevent someone who correctly guesses your policy from confirming the guess.** Membership privacy is protected by not publishing, not by cryptography, and no product copy may say otherwise.

### LP-9 — Personal-View and citation disclosure defaults

**Example.** You quote a file in a paper. Should the link also carry *which of your trusted sources* selected that file, so a stranger can reproduce your exact view — or just the file and the moment?
**Options.** (a) **Split the forms:** an object/claim citation carries the pinned object and basis and **no policy reference** (this is the frequent act, and it needs none); a *view* citation carries the policy and therefore carries the disclosure preview and is a deliberate act. ### LP-10 — Computed membership: may a machine-derived trust list be *followed*?

**Example.** Your own achievement idea makes this concrete: an app publishes "everyone who earned achievement X," signed, with evidence — and publishes it as a **channel** that advances hourly. A user subscribes once and thereafter an unbounded, machine-derived set of strangers refreshes inside their compiled policy, without a per-revision decision. The system's one fence against web-of-trust scoring ("published explicit policy, deliberately imported") passes this case, because a channel produces a signed revision on every advance.
**Options.** (a) **Distinguish curated from computed:** a published policy declares `derivationKind: CURATED | COMPUTED(algorithmRef)`; a COMPUTED set is **pin-only** (never channel-followed), needs an explicit per-adoption ceremony to hold an authority tier, and counts at full cardinality against the effective-principal budget. (b) Allow computed sets to be followed with disclosure. (c) Draw no distinction (the fence stands as written).
**Rec: (a), medium-high confidence.** Nostr's web-of-trust experiment produced no reproducible score, unsolved Sybil exposure, and centralization into scoring services — field evidence for the exclusion, not a hypothesis. And this decision is the guardrail your own Ideas entry asks for when it worries about "turning any of them into a generic green trust badge."
