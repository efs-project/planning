## What this is

James's target experience: *"You click Mac OS 7 and a real little computer running in your browser pops up."* This pass investigates the strongest existing OS-preservation product — the [Virtual OS Museum](https://virtualosmuseum.org/) — plus the entire browser-emulation field, and pressure-tests EFS v2's generic design against the experience. It extends [[playable-archive-requirements]] rather than starting a second archive design.

## The museum, in plain language

The Virtual OS Museum is one Canadian developer, **Andrew Warkentin**, who spent 20+ years (since 2003) collecting, installing, configuring, patching, and documenting old operating systems, and in May 2026 shipped the collection as a **downloadable virtual machine**: a Debian Linux guest you run under QEMU, VirtualBox, or UTM. Inside it: a Qt launcher, **1,700+ pre-installed OS installations across 250+ platforms and ~570–600 distinct OSes, spanning 1948 to now** — Multics, PDP-7 Unix, Xerox Alto, Lisa OS, every classic Mac and Windows line including leaked betas, NeXTSTEP, early Android, and hundreds of systems most people have never heard of — with working logins, documentation, screenshots, and often period software. Full edition: 130 GB zipped. Lite edition: 14 GB, with per-installation download-on-demand.

How it actually works is refreshingly boring, and that's its genius: everything — launcher, metadata, emulators, disk images — is packaged as **~3,300 ordinary Debian packages** served from a GPG-signed apt repository; "download on demand" is literally `apt-get install`. Metadata is plain-text `Key: value` files. Installations pin **exact emulator versions** where regressions demand it (~150+ emulators, some patched, several only working in specific versions because later ones regressed). Every machine directory is a **btrfs subvolume** with automatic `initial` snapshots, so one click reverts a broken system. Distribution is BitTorrent plus the Internet Archive.

**Why it succeeded where archives stay hard to use:** the thesis on its homepage — "available somewhere" is not "one click and actually works." Between a disk image on an archive site and a running system stand install procedures, device configuration, emulator-version regressions, and host-environment quirks. The museum's whole value is that *one person already fought all of that* and froze the result. It took him roughly 2.5 years to get from "I have the bytes" to "you can run them" — and at launch only about half the 1,700 installs had been re-verified. Executable preservation is an ongoing verb, not a state.

**Its fragility:** bus factor 1, by its own description. The curator is the sole committer, uploader, tester, takedown contact, and holder of an *unpublished* apt signing key and patched-emulator sources that are almost all unpublished ("coming soon"; two forks excepted), funded by Patreon (17 patrons) and income support. The launcher/metadata are **source-available but noncommercial** (old-MAME license + CC BY-NC-SA) — not open source under OSD or FSF criteria. The guest images are mostly **unlicensed commercial abandonware** on a screening-plus-takedown forbearance posture. And the museum's own distribution is hosted on the Internet Archive — it is a *tenant* of the IA model, not an alternative. If the curator vanishes: the frozen bytes survive (torrents, IA, published SHA256s — genuinely good exit properties); the *institution* — updates, testing, judgment, takedown handling, the image backlog that would grow the collection to 2,000–3,000 installations — does not.

One more fact that frames everything: the curator **considered and rejected browser delivery** — too many of his emulators have no web port, and the launcher stack would need a parallel implementation. He's right about his stack. That rejected lane is exactly the seam EFS would fill.

## Which OS classes can actually run in a browser

Detailed 23-row matrix: [`feasibility-matrix.md`](./2026-07-29-virtual-os-museum-corpus/feasibility-matrix.md). The compressed truth:

- **Browser-native-practical today:** DOS, Windows 3.x–98, classic 68k/PPC Mac (System 1 through Mac OS 9), NeXTSTEP, Amiga, the 8-bit micros, PDP-class minis, PalmOS, DOS gaming — via small (0.2–7 MB), mostly GPL/BSD/MIT WASM/JS engines (v86, Infinite Mac's cores, vAmigaWeb, JSSpeccy, js-dos, PCjs, CloudpilotEmu). The first-hand probe measured the flagship case: **Infinite Mac boots System 7.0 on ~11–12 MB decoded — 2.2 MB of engine, ROM and glue plus exactly 9.00 MB of on-demand disk chunks against a 40 MB image — reaching a settled desktop 1.9 s after emulator start** (the project's own cold-cache figure is ~3 s). Disk images ship as KB-scale chunk manifests — the System 7 boot disk's is 1 KB — over 256 KB content-addressed chunks, sparse-aware, streamed on demand: a shape within an inch of EFS's `FileManifest` draft ([`hands-on-test-log.md`](./2026-07-29-virtual-os-museum-corpus/hands-on-test-log.md)).
- **Capable with limits:** Windows 2000/XP, Mac OS X 10.0–10.4, Atari ST, arcade, consoles, early Android, and modern Linux (the only fully-legal modern row).
- **Not in browsers, honestly:** IBM mainframes (no Hercules wasm port was found), commercial Unix workstations (Solaris/IRIX/HP-UX/AIX — only partially emulated even natively), modern Windows/macOS (technical + licensing walls). These are local-native or server-stream territory, and hundreds of VOM's 250+ platforms have no browser emulator at all.
- **The reality checks the mission demanded:** WASI is a capability interface for compiled programs, not hardware virtualization — the full-system cores that need a POSIX-ish OS layer (Infinite Mac's cores, qemu-wasm, em-dosbox) use Emscripten rather than WASI, the lighter cores are plain JS/Rust/AssemblyScript, and container2wasm's WASI builds work by hosting a CPU emulator *on* a WASI runtime. A Docker/OCI image cannot execute in a browser; its honest roles are reproducible build input, server deployment, and conversion input (container2wasm wraps a CPU emulator *inside* the wasm — packaging innovation, not execution). "Every OS runs in the browser" is **falsified**; "the iconic consumer-computing core runs beautifully in the browser" is **verified**.
- **The two constraints that actually decide architecture:** (1) **media rights** — the legally clean set is FreeDOS, Linux, Ancient UNIX V1–V7/32V (Caldera grant), ZX Spectrum/CPC (Amstrad's blanket ROM permission), AROS/EmuTOS replacement ROMs, Android-x86, and homebrew; everything Apple/Microsoft runs on *tolerated* distribution (Infinite Mac commits Apple ROMs to a public repo, no legality statement, no takedown in 4 years — tolerated ≠ licensed), while Commodore system ROMs are commercially licensed (Cloanto), which pushes clean deployments to AROS/open-roms replacements; (2) **cross-origin isolation** — the field splits cleanly into engines needing no SharedArrayBuffer/COOP/COEP (v86, js-dos, all the 8-bit/mini cores — embeddable anywhere immediately) and SAB-class engines (Infinite Mac's cores, qemu-wasm, WebVM) that need isolation headers on first paint, which collides with clientv2's current repeat-load-only header injection.

## Verdict: can EFS reach functional parity?

**Yes — on the axis that matters, with honestly bounded breadth.** Parity with VOM's *experience* (click → configured machine boots, snapshots, docs, credentials, curated catalog) is achievable in-browser for the popular core of computing history, and the hands-on probe shows the per-exhibit marginal cost is megabytes and seconds, not gigabytes and an install. Parity with VOM's *breadth* (250+ platforms, native-speed fidelity, mainframes and workstations) is **not** achievable in-browser and should never be claimed; those classes enter as metadata-only records, BYO-media, native-runner companions, or later server-stream tiers.

The architecture-pressure result ([`efs-coverage-gap-ledger.md`](./2026-07-29-virtual-os-museum-corpus/efs-coverage-gap-ledger.md), 16 checks): **no museum-specific protocol surface is needed.** Every capability maps to generic draft primitives — DATA identities, FileManifest byte commitments, releases + PIN channel heads, claims + lenses, package closures, guest links. The pressure lands on three *already-named* pressure points (two spec debts plus the economics gate), one client decision (the runner lane), and one unwritten client design (the emulator adapter) — not on new contract surface. The boundary rule in [[playable-archive-requirements]] ("no contract field or read method unless a named acceptance test fails on generic primitives") survives this pass intact — with sharper acceptance tests than before.

## Verdict: where can EFS be materially better?

1. **The funnel.** VOM: install a hypervisor, download 14–130 GB, boot a VM. EFS: follow a link, see context, click Play — ~2.5 MB critical path for a System-7-class exhibit. This is not an increment; it's the difference between an enthusiast artifact and a shareable web object. It's also the exact lane the curator rejected as infeasible *for his stack*.
2. **Mobile and reach.** VOM needs a desktop, and its guest VM is x86-only (ARM hosts run it under emulation, slowly). The browser-native tier runs on phones today (vc64web, vAmigaWeb, CloudpilotEmu are PWAs).
3. **Verified bytes from interchangeable mirrors.** VOM's trust chain bottoms out in an unpublished GPG key and curator assertion. EFS generations are content-committed, mirror-agnostic, and independently checkable — `CONTENT-MISMATCH` instead of silent corruption.
4. **Plural curation without forking the collection.** VOM cannot express "two curators disagree whether this install works." Lens-scoped claims can — compatibility, rights, safety, and playability as separate attributable facts.
5. **Institutional survivability.** VOM's bytes outlive its curator, but its institution doesn't. EFS's design (permanent records, permissionless re-pinning, walk-away export, chains-don't-die ruling) makes the *catalog itself* the survivable object — including surviving EFS-the-company, which VOM-analog institutions cannot claim.
6. **Citable, reproducible exhibits.** A generation name is a permanent link to an exact runnable state — "the machine as configured on this date" as a citation. VOM's dated releases already give collection-level reproducibility; EFS adds per-exhibit granularity, content-addressed verification, and permanence.
7. **Runtime preservation.** Archiving exact emulator generations *with source bundles* fixes the field's own bus-factor problem (VOM's mostly-unpublished patches; Infinite Mac's one-person fork farm; JSLinux's unreleased engine).

**Where VOM stays better, permanently:** breadth (hundreds of platforms with no web emulator), native-speed fidelity, twenty years of installation/configuration knowledge, tested oddball peripherals (card punches, terminal servers, PLATO), and a working curator. None of that is replaceable by protocol design, and the honest posture is to treat VOM as a *complementary upstream and import source* — respecting its CC BY-NC-SA metadata license — not a rival to replace.

## What cannot honestly be promised

- **Not "every OS."** Mainframes, workstations, modern proprietary OSes: no browser path; hundreds of VOM platforms out of scope.
- **Not "Mac OS 7 for everyone" without a rights decision.** The flagship demo class runs on tolerated Apple bytes everywhere it exists today. EFS's ruled permanence (**no protocol takedown**) makes the VOM/IA forbearance posture *structurally riskier* for EFS: an infringing image on permanent tiers cannot be removed, only unlisted and lens-filtered. Strict-clean, BYO-media, and revocable-mirror-tier-only postures all work; silent adoption of the tolerated posture does not. NEEDS-COUNSEL items are registered in the [licensing lane](./2026-07-29-virtual-os-museum-corpus/licensing-and-neutrality.md).
- **Not zero maintenance.** VOM's numbers are the ground truth: half-tested at launch, breakage within weeks, emulator regressions as the top rot source. EFS makes test evidence cheap, plural, and attributable — it does not make testing free.
- **Not native-speed everything, not determinism.** Browser emulation is fine-to-great for the practical tier; no surveyed runtime documents cross-version deterministic replay (MAME's version-pinned input recording is the closest), so citation claims must scope to save-states.
- **Not "hosts the bytes on-chain" yet.** The gas snapshot (freeze-gates A2) is unproduced; until the economics verdict exists, the claim is "EFS names, verifies, and locates OS images across mirrors," not "EFS hosts them."

## Existing requirements confirmed

This pass **confirms** [[playable-archive-requirements]] as written on: the Browse→Inspect→Preflight→Play→Return journey; explicit Play (Infinite Mac's auto-boot-on-navigation is the anti-pattern for hyperlinks, acceptable only for curated embeds); PAF-2 exact generations with exact runtime generations (VOM's `Emulator-Version:` pinning is 20 years of evidence this is *the* load-bearing metadata); PAF-3 transport-agnostic verified loading; PAF-5's three host profiles and capability ceilings; PAF-6 overlay-shaped local saves with eviction honesty (observed shipping in Infinite Mac, dirty-chunk OPFS overlay and cross-tab lock dialog included); PAF-7's separated facts and metadata-only records; PAF-8 idempotent publishing; and the protocol boundary rule itself.

## Requirements that need tightening (proposed edits, not made)

1. **PAF-3/PAF-2 — streamable vs. execute-critical closure entries.** Clientv2's all-or-nothing closure staging kills the 2-second boot if applied to GB-scale disk images. The manifest should distinguish *execute-critical* (verify before run) from *streamable* (chunk-verified on demand) entries; a missing streamed chunk is an in-emulator I/O error, never a half-verified app.
2. **PAF-3 — range reads become load-bearing.** "May use streaming" should become a hard requirement for emulator-backed packages, tied to the range-read spec the 2026-07-22 mount ruling already demands and large-file-uploads doesn't yet contain.
3. **PAF-5 — isolation profile declaration.** Packages must declare SAB/COOP-COEP needs; preflight must handle the first-load isolation gap explicitly (isolated archive origin, header lane, or unsupported-on-this-load honesty). Sequencing note: an entire excellent exhibit tier needs no isolation at all.
4. **PAF-6 — bless the overlay save shape.** Immutable base + dirty-chunk overlay, isolated per generation, exportable — now field-proven.
5. **PAF-7 — smoke-test claim tuple.** Pin the convention: (tester, client generation, runtime generation, browser/version, date, result). VOM's `Confirmed-Working` + one tester is the cautionary baseline.
6. **PAF-2/PAF-7 — runtime source bundles.** An emulator generation should carry its exact source tree and build recipe (OCI image as *build input*). "Sources coming soon" is the failure mode this guards against.
7. **Boot budgets — a large-app warming tier.** Ideas' guest-link modularity rule plus BOOT's ≤1.2 MiB budget cover the viewer; there is no defined budget/UX tier for an `a`-link whose app legitimately needs tens of MB. Define the progressive-warming state machine rather than letting emulator exhibits silently blow the budget.

## Client / SDK / tooling work needed (gap list)

- **Client:** player shell (PAF-4) with the emulator adapter bridge v0 (screen/input/disk/audio/save over MessagePort); the runner-lane evidence spike (canvas-mode SES worker hosting v86-class cores vs. sandboxed-iframe compat lane); capability additions that simply don't exist yet — **audio, fullscreen, pointer-lock, gamepad**, timing-precision policy; `render.canvas` guest-floor placement; COOP/COEP strategy for SAB-class runtimes; a11y answer for emulated bitmap screens (the semantic-sidecar rule is currently unsatisfiable for a 1984 Mac desktop — see [`open-questions.md`](./2026-07-29-virtual-os-museum-corpus/open-questions.md) A7).
- **SDK:** FileManifest + chunked verified range reads; closure resolve/verify; idempotent publisher with pin reuse; CAR-style export/import; storage-worker throughput validation at emulator disk-I/O rates.
- **Tooling/content:** manifest schema for machine definitions (VOM info files and PCjs machine XML are the precedents); smoke-test harness and claim publisher; facts-only importers (VOM, IA items); rights-screening pipeline that treats firmware as first-class rights-bearing entries.

## Protocol gaps

**None proven.** Three pressure points sharpened (all pre-existing): generation naming (DATA-identity vs. manifest-bytes reconciliation — PAF open question 1), range-read semantics (spec text over the existing chunk model, with P11's per-chunk SHA-256/SRI word riding along as retrofit-painful), and bulk-byte economics (gas-snapshot-gated). Their falsification tests are A1, A2, and A9 in [`open-questions.md`](./2026-07-29-virtual-os-museum-corpus/open-questions.md); if any test fails on generic primitives, that failure — not this review — becomes the protocol argument.

## Staged demonstration plan

Full plan with acceptance tests A1–A8: [`vertical-slice.md`](./2026-07-29-virtual-os-museum-corpus/vertical-slice.md). Shape: **Stage 0** static spike (FreeDOS on v86 + Unix V7 on a PDP-11/70 + ZX Spectrum homebrew — a 100 % legally-clean trio spanning the runtime space); **Stage 1** EFS-published generations on devnet (naming, bounded reads, corrupted-chunk mismatch test, idempotent publish); **Stage 2** guest-link player shell in client v2 (isolation, saves, explicit Play); **Stage 3** plural curation with dissenting claims; **Stage 4** the emotional headline done honestly — BYO-media Mac OS 7 against a metadata-only record, nothing tolerated ever distributed; **Stage 5** facts-only VOM/IA import proof. The slice *fails loudly* into a design handoff if any acceptance test can't be met with generic primitives.
