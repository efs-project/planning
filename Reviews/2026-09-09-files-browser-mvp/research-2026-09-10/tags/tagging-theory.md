# Tagging theory and pluralistic labeling — research report for the EFS v2 tag deep-dive

**Date:** 2026-09-10. **Method:** vault read (file:line cited), then primary sources on the web (URL + date). Gas figures are tagged MEASURED / QUOTED / ESTIMATED as in the gas and indexing reports. Where a primary source could not be fetched, that is stated rather than papered over.

---

## 0. The headline finding, stated first

Every successful labeling system separates **the concept's identity** from **the strings people see**, and treats **hierarchy as an editable claim over stable ids**, not as part of the id. The systems that baked the name (or the parent path) into the identity — LCSH, Wikipedia categories, macOS Finder tags, Obsidian nested tags, Danbooru (before aliases) — each paid for it with "rename = rewrite every record", bots with millions of edits, or multi-year political stalls.

The vault currently holds **both** shapes and they conflict:

- `Designs/efsv2/fable-handoff-v2-tag-core.md:70` — `tagId = keccak(DOMAIN, parentTagId, keccak(name), kind)`; rename = REDIRECT over immutable ids. The name **and** the parent are inside the identity. This is the LCSH/Wikipedia-category shape with an on-chain redirect layer bolted on.
- `Designs/media-library/booru-app.md:128-137` — `TagConcept` "contains a stable concept identity and namespace, not one canonical English string"; labels, translations, aliases, implications, categories are "separate authored records". This is the SKOS/Wikidata shape.
- `Designs/efsv2/layered-type-system-and-data-abi.md:707-709` already rules, for Types, that "a path must not be the Type's canonical identity", and `:741-751` that "renaming or moving a catalog entry does not break exact Records". `:766-768` names the principle: immutable hashes are true references, human names are mutable metadata.

The evidence below says the Type rule should apply to tags, and that paths (which must resolve deterministically) and tag-concepts (which must survive renames, merges and translation) are two different objects that the "paths are tags" collapse conflates. Details and the recommended shape are in §8.

## 1. Folksonomies — what the research actually established

**Mathes (Dec 2004), "Folksonomies — Cooperative Classification and Communication Through Shared Metadata"** (https://adammathes.com/academic/computer-mediated-communication/folksonomies.html). Named the defects that every later paper measured: ambiguity ("filtering" = water filters vs Bayesian), synonyms/plurals ("mac/macintosh/apple", "flower/flowers"), a flat namespace with "no directly specified parent-child or sibling relationships", and hypothesised a power-law tag distribution. Merholz's "desire lines" reading: use emergent tags to *derive* a controlled vocabulary rather than impose one.

**Golder & Huberman (2006), J. Information Science 32(2):198–208; arXiv cs/0508082 (Aug 2005)** (https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0508082; full text extracted from the arXiv PDF). Del.icio.us, 19,422 bookmarks and n=229 users. Findings that matter here:
- Seven tag functions: (1) what/who it is about, (2) what it is (`article`, `blog`), (3) who owns it, (4) refining categories (round numbers like `25`, `100`), (5) qualities (`funny`, `scary`), (6) self reference (`mystuff`), (7) task organizing (`toread`, `jobsearch`). Categories 5–7 are per-user, not shared vocabulary — a pluralistic system should expect and isolate them (Suchanek below calls them non-"meaningful").
- The three problems: polysemy, synonymy, and **basic-level variation** ("cat" vs "animal" vs "beagle" depending on expertise) — the last one is exactly the "/clouds/nimbus vs Nimbus" question: different users tag at different depths of the same hierarchy.
- Stability: "usually after the first 100 or so bookmarks, each tag's frequency is a nearly fixed proportion of the total" (§4.2). Explained by a Pólya urn; two causes named — **imitation** (the Delicious UI shows the tags others already used) and **shared knowledge** (the pattern holds even for tags the UI does *not* show).

**Marlow, Naaman, boyd, Davis (HT06, 2006)** (https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/1149941.1149949; only the abstract was reachable) — the design-dimension taxonomy (tagging rights, tagging support blind/viewable/suggestive, aggregation bag-vs-set). I could not fetch the full text; treat the dimension list as from memory of the paper, not verified here.

**Halpin, Robu, Shepherd (WWW 2007), "The Complex Dynamics of Collaborative Tagging"** (https://archives.iw3c2.org/www2007/papers/paper635.pdf; full text extracted). 500 popular del.icio.us sites tagged "close to 2000 times", top 25 tags each → power law; a second sample of 500 recent sites (mean 286 users) mostly noise "though … a power law is beginning to emerge". Convergence measured with Kullback-Leibler divergence between monthly rank-frequency vectors: "Convergence to zero occurs at approximately the same time period, often within a few months". Generative story: preferential attachment (users reinforce tags they see) — and they explicitly note the 25-tag cut-off "may be an artifact of the user interface … receive a particular number of suggestions". Follow-up: Robu, Halpin, Shepherd, ACM TWEB 3(4), 2009 (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/39998376_Emergence_of_Consensus_and_Shared_Vocabularies_in_Collaborative_Tagging_Systems).

**Cattuto, Loreto, Pietronero (PNAS 104:1461, 2007; arXiv cs/0605015 May 2006)** (https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0605015). Yule-Simon-type model with "(i) a frequency-bias mechanism … users are exposed to each other's tagging activity and (ii) a notion of memory … heavy-tailed access to the past state". Same conclusion from a physics angle: the stable vocabulary is produced by exposure to prior tags.

**Suchanek, Vojnovic, Gunawardena (CIKM 2008), "Social tags: meaning and suggestions"** (https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1458082.1458114). User study with >4,000 participants: "roughly one third of the tag applications may be induced by the suggestions"; the more popular tags of a page tend to be the more "meaningful" ones (identify the item or a characteristic, as opposed to organisational/self-reference tags).

**Kipp (ASIST 2006), "Patterns and Inconsistencies in Collaborative Tagging Systems"** (https://asistdl.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/meet.14504301178) — inconsistency catalogue (spelling, form, affective tags). Abstract only.

**What stabilises free text (the operational answer):** (a) showing existing tags at write time — imitation is the dominant mechanism in every dataset; (b) suggestions/autocomplete — ~⅓ of applications; (c) friction on *creating* a new term — Stack Overflow requires 1,500 reputation to create a tag and made synonym remapping "automatic and silent" (blog, 1 Aug 2010, https://stackoverflow.blog/2010/08/01/tag-folksonomy-and-tag-synonyms/); (d) a documented-not-enforced convention layer — OpenStreetMap: "You can use any tags you like", "please document them here on the wiki", proposals optional, and a warning against "unilaterally changing the definition of existing tags" (https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Any_tags_you_like). Nothing in the literature shows a *contract-level* vocabulary constraint mattering; convergence is a UI and social effect.

**Implication for EFS:** the per-segment TAGDEF record cost (`fable-handoff:71`) is itself a Stack-Overflow-style creation friction, and autocomplete "from the Lens's existing TAGDEFs" is the imitation channel. Those two are the vocabulary-control mechanism. Per-user tag functions 5–7 should live under the user's own principal, not the shared namespace — which the Lens model already gives you for free.

## 2. Controlled vocabularies and thesauri

### SKOS (W3C Recommendation, 18 Aug 2009) — https://www.w3.org/TR/skos-reference/ ; Primer https://www.w3.org/TR/skos-primer/

The concept/label separation is the whole design:
- A concept is "an idea or notion; a unit of thought" identified by URI (§3). Labels are plain literals with language tags: `prefLabel` / `altLabel` / `hiddenLabel` (§5). Integrity condition **S14**: at most one `prefLabel` per language. SKOS-XL (Primer §4.3) reifies labels into resources with their own URIs when you need relations *between labels* (acronym-of, translation-of) — i.e. labels can themselves be first-class records when required, but they are never the identity.
- **Hierarchy is deliberately untyped.** `broader` = "in some way more general" (§8.1). Primer §2.3.1: "Not covered in basic SKOS is the distinction between types of hierarchical relation: for example, instance-class and part-whole relationships." `broader`/`narrower` are *not* transitive (S-conditions, Example 34); `broaderTransitive` exists only for inference (query expansion), not assertion. `related` is symmetric, not transitive, and **disjoint with `broaderTransitive` (S27)** — a clash between hierarchical and associative links is "not consistent with the SKOS data model".
- **Mapping ≠ merging.** `exactMatch` is symmetric and transitive (S44, S45), a sub-property of `closeMatch` (S42), and disjoint with `broadMatch`/`relatedMatch` (S46). Primer §3.1 on why not `owl:sameAs`: "triples involving these resources are merged. This does not fit what is needed in most SKOS applications" — two vocabularies asserting the same concept must keep separate prefLabels and scheme memberships. This is the read-time-alias position, formalised.
- **Concept schemes have no closed boundary** (§4.6.1: "There is no way to close the boundary of a concept scheme"); one concept can be in many schemes; `ConceptScheme` is disjoint with `Concept` (S9).
- **Groupers are not concepts.** `skos:Collection` / `OrderedCollection` (§9) exist for thesaurus "node labels"/"guide terms". Primer §4.1: "There is consensus that a node label does **not** represent a label for a concept in its own right." Collections are disjoint with Concepts (S37) and **cannot be linked by broader/narrower** (§9.6.4). This is the owner's "clouds grouper" exactly: SKOS says a grouper is a different kind of object, with members, and is *not* the broader concept.
- `skos:notation` (§6) is a typed literal for scheme-local codes ("T58.5"); uniqueness is convention, not enforced (§6.5.3).

### ISO 25964-1 (2011) and the typed-hierarchy problem
ISO 25964 splits BT/NT into **BTG** (generic, is-a), **BTP** (partitive, part-of) and **BTI** (instantial, instance-of). Alexiev, Isaac, Lindenthal (2015), "On the composition of ISO 25964 hierarchical relations (BTG, BTP, BTI)" (https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/on-the-composition-of-iso-25964-hierarchical-relations-btg-btp-bt/): "specialized relationship types cannot be arbitrarily combined to produce new statements that have the same semantic precision"; mixing them in one broader chain yields misleading transitive inferences; worked out against the Getty AAT. The practical rule: if you offer transitive "is X under /pizza" walks (`fable-handoff:70`), the edge type must be known, or the walk is only safe for a single homogeneous relation.

### Library of Congress Subject Headings — the cost of string-as-identity
LCSH headings are pre-coordinated strings, and MARC bibliographic records carry the **string** in 650 fields, not the authority id. The "Illegal aliens" case (Wikipedia summary with dated citations: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_aliens_(Library_of_Congress_Subject_Heading); CCS cataloging manual table: https://www.ccslib.org/Catalogers/index.php?title=Immigration_Subject_Headings; ARLIS statement: https://www.arlisna.org/news/arlisna-statement-on-removal-of-library-of-congress---illegal-aliens---subject-headings):
- 2014 Dartmouth request → March 2016 LC decision to cancel → **June 2016 Congress blocked it via an appropriations rider** → 12 Nov 2021 LC announced "Aliens"→"Noncitizens", "Illegal aliens"→"Illegal immigration" (+ "Noncitizens") → implemented Dec 2021. Seven years for one rename.
- The rename cascaded through the *pre-coordinated* family: "Alien detention centers", "Children of illegal aliens", "Illegal alien children", "Women illegal aliens", "Illegal aliens in literature" all changed — because the parent string is embedded in each child string.
- Over forty libraries forked locally before LC moved (Colorado 2018, CSU 2020, Harvard 2021…). CCS instructs catalogers to "update the records by replacing the LCSH terms" — a per-record string rewrite.
- **Could not verify:** id.loc.gov (authority record `sh85066343`) and the LC decision PDFs returned HTTP 403, so I cannot quote whether the authority control number was retained with the new heading or cancelled. Do not cite the sh-number as fact from this report.

### Faceted classification
- Ranganathan, *Colon Classification* (Madras Library Association, 1933): the first analytico-synthetic scheme; PMEST facets (Personality, Matter, Energy, Space, Time) combined by a facet formula instead of one enumerated tree (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colon_classification; https://www.historyofinformation.com/detail.php?id=4384).
- Kwasnik (1999), "The Role of Classification in Knowledge Representation and Discovery", *Library Trends* 48(1):22–47 (https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ595482): compares hierarchies, trees, paradigms and facets by their ability to reflect and create knowledge. The PDF was 403; the abstract confirms the four-structure frame. The standard reading (not verified against the text here): hierarchies assume inheritance/transitivity/mutual exclusivity and complete domain knowledge; trees (part-whole) have no inheritance; facets are "hospitable" to new items without restructuring. The booru namespace list in `booru-app.md:141-144` (artist, character, species, medium, rating…) is a facet scheme, and Danbooru's tag *categories* are facets; the "clouds/nimbus" example is a hierarchy inside one facet. Both are needed; neither is a path.

## 3. Wikidata and the Wikipedia category graph

### Wikidata — id vs label vs alias, and what merge/redirect look like at scale
- Items are Q-ids; labels are per-language; aliases are per-language "Also known as", **not unique** — the only uniqueness rule is label+description per language (Help:Aliases, https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Help:Aliases). Aliases are search keys, never identifiers.
- Hierarchy properties (Help:Basic membership properties, https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Help:Basic_membership_properties): **P31 instance-of is not transitive; P279 subclass-of is transitive; P361 part-of is transitive.** The stated primary error is using P31 where P279 is meant.
- The ontology is fought over continuously. WikiProject Ontology/Problems (https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:WikiProject_Ontology/Problems): "Ontologies should generally be tree-like … the resulting graph should be acyclic"; automated reports track P279 cycles up to 6th order and items that are instances of themselves; the Central Park example (instance of both `urban park` and `park`, with `urban park` itself an instance of `park`); classes with >1000 direct subclasses flagged. Lesson: even with typed relations and stable ids, a permissionless hierarchy accumulates cycles and level-confusion, so *readers must tolerate cycles* — which `tags-maximalist.md:90` already concedes ("multi-hop resolution, SCC lowest-id tie-break, depth caps were never on-chain").
- **Merge** (Help:Merge, https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Help:Merge): recipient is usually the older/lower Q-id; statements, labels, sitelinks move; the obsolete item **becomes a redirect**. Sitelink conflicts block merges, in which case P460 "said to be the same as" is recorded until the wikis merge — a soft "sameAs" claim rather than a hard merge.
- **Redirects** (Help:Redirects, https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Help:Redirects): "Under no circumstances should redirects be deleted or repurposed for another object. Deleting them would mean invalidating possible references." Statements that point at the old id are rewritten by a bot (KrBot) "within a few days"; double redirects are collapsed by Revibot; the query service exposes redirects as `owl:sameAs`. Undoing a merge requires restoring revisions **and** the bot owner's help because the bot has already rewritten references. History is retained (revision history of both items).

### Wikipedia categories — identity = page title, hierarchy = a graph with cycles
- A category is identified by its page title in the Category namespace (Help:Category, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Category). Subcategorisation "makes it possible to organize categories into tree-like structures". Moving a category page does **not** move members: "a certain amount of cleanup may be necessary"; the old name gets `{{category redirect}}` (a soft redirect), and members are moved by a bot after the CfD 48-hour wait (Wikipedia:Categories for discussion, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Categories_for_discussion). Cydebot did this for ~6.4 million edits and is now listed inactive (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/User:Cydebot). Wikipedia:Categorization notes the cost directly: a rename "imposes an additional overhead – an edit for *every* categorized page" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Categorization).
- Grouper categories exist as a distinct kind: `{{Container category}}` — "This is a container category. Due to its scope, it should contain *only* subcategories" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Template:Container_category). Also set categories (plural, "Cities in France") vs topic categories (singular, "France") vs eponymous categories.
- Graph shape: Zesch & Gurevych (TextGraphs/NAACL 2007, https://aclanthology.org/W07-0201.pdf; text extracted): German WCG snapshot 15 May 2006 — 27,865 category nodes, γ≈2.12, average path length 7.18, diameter 17, clustering 0.012; scale-free small-world like WordNet. "As Wikipedia does not strictly enforce a taxonomic category structure, cycles and disconnected categories are possible, but rare" — the largest component had 99.8% of nodes "as well as 7 cycles". A widely repeated claim that English Wikipedia has cycles of length 22 and Hindustani/Urdu mutual sub-categorisation surfaced only in search-engine summaries; **I could not locate a citable page for it** and do not rely on it.

## 4. Pluralistic labeling — the deployed analogues to Lenses

### Bluesky / AT Protocol labels (the closest deployed analogue)
Spec: https://atproto.com/specs/label ; lexicons: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bluesky-social/atproto/main/lexicons/com/atproto/label/defs.json and `app/bsky/labeler/defs.json` ; docs: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bluesky-social/bsky-docs/main/docs/advanced-guides/moderation.md ; client logic: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bluesky-social/atproto/main/packages/api/src/moderation/decision.ts ; launch post "Bluesky's Stackable Approach to Moderation", 12 Mar 2024 (https://bsky.social/about/blog/03-12-2024-stackable-moderation).

- **Label = (src DID, uri, val, cts) + optional cid, neg, exp, sig, ver=1.** `val` ≤128 bytes, recommended lowercase a–z with dashes; `!` prefix marks system-behaviour labels (`!hide`, `!warn`, `!no-unauthenticated`). Targets: accounts (`did:`) or records (`at://`), optionally pinned to a `cid` (a specific version). Self-labels are embedded in the record (max 10) and are restricted to `!no-unauthenticated, porn, sexual, nudity, graphic-media, bot`.
- **Negation is retraction, not denial.** Publish the same (src, uri, val) with `neg=true` and a later `cts`; "a label with value spam and neg true does not mean the subject is not spam, only that a previous spam label should be disregarded". Expiry via `exp`.
- **Vocabulary is per-labeler, not global.** A labeler publishes `policies.labelValues` and `labelValueDefinitions` — "Label values created by this labeler and scoped exclusively to it. Labels defined here will override global label definitions for this labeler." A definition = `identifier`, `severity` (inform|alert|none), `blurs` (content|media|none), `defaultSetting` (ignore|warn|hide), `adultOnly`, `locales[{lang,name,description}]`. So the same string `nudity` from two labelers is two definitions; the string is just a key scoped by `src`. **No hierarchy, no aliases, no implications** exist in the protocol.
- **Composition:** the client sends `atproto-accept-labelers` (a list of DIDs); the AppView returns only those labelers' labels. Up to 20 labelers; "The Bluesky application hardcodes Bluesky's moderation for inclusion, leaving 19 additional subscriptions to the users." Each user sets a per-labeler, per-label preference (ignore/warn/hide). `decision.ts` then assigns a priority per cause — 1 for `no-override` or adult-with-adult-disabled, 2 for user pref `hide`, 5 for blur of profile/content view, 7 for blur of list/media, 8 for inform — merges causes from all labelers, and the UI sorts by priority: **the most restrictive behaviour wins; there is no ordering among labelers.** Labels are behaviours with severities, so union-then-max is the right combinator for them.
- Distribution: `com.atproto.label.subscribeLabels` (cursor from 0) and `queryLabels`; labels are signed, so they are portable claims. Ozone is the open-source labeler.

**Contrast with the EFS Lens** (ordered trusted principals, first-attester-wins, `fable-handoff:33`): Bluesky deliberately does *not* pick a first winner — because two labelers rarely disagree in a way that needs a winner; they add up. For *bindings* (one value per position) first-wins is necessary; for *tags* (cardinality-N claims, `tags-maximalist.md:38`) the Bluesky combinator — union with provenance, then a per-viewer policy — matches what `visual-gallery-and-booru-ecosystems.md:157-168` demands ("who said what", "visible disagreement"). The Lens needs a second combinator for card-N claims; today the docs only define the card-1 one.

### ActivityPub / Mastodon hashtags
Mastodon's ActivityPub doc (https://docs.joinmastodon.org/spec/activitypub/): a hashtag is `{type: "Hashtag", name: "#cats", href: …}` in the post's `tag` array; "Mastodon will also normalize hashtags to be case-insensitive lowercase strings, performing ASCII folding and removing invalid characters." No hierarchy, no ids — the normalized string *is* the identity, per instance. Actors expose `featuredTags` (a Collection of Hashtag objects) for profile discovery. Following hashtags shipped in 4.0 (Nov 2022; https://mastodon.social/@Mastodon/109344018834782353). Pluralism here is purely per-instance (each server owns its hashtag pages); there is no cross-instance vocabulary object at all.

### Nostr NIP-32 labels
https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/32.md — kind 1985; `["L", namespace]` + `["l", value, namespace]`; targets `e`/`p`/`a`/`r`/`t`; the `ugc` namespace is implied when absent; reverse-domain namespaces encouraged; any pubkey may label anything; self-labels allowed in any event. **No hierarchy, no negation, no retraction in the NIP** (deletion is generic NIP-09, not label-aware). Namespacing by *labeler-chosen namespace string* instead of by labeler identity is the weaker design: two labelers using `ISO-639-1` are presumed compatible with nothing enforcing it.

## 5. Hierarchical tags in tools — identity-bearing or display grouping?

| Tool | Hierarchy representation | Identity-bearing? | Rename a parent → | Source (date) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Obsidian** | `#inbox/to-read`; `tag:inbox` matches all nested; case-insensitive; stored as inline text or YAML `tags` list | **Yes** — the full path string is the tag | No core rename; the Tag Wrangler community plugin does find-and-replace across the vault | https://obsidian.md/help/tags ; https://github.com/pjeby/tag-wrangler |
| **Notion** | select/multi_select options: `id` ("Does not change if the name is changed"), `name`, `color`; **no nesting**; `status` property has one level of *groups* over `option_ids` | No — id is identity, grouping is a separate list | Rename is free; groups are reconfigured independently | https://developers.notion.com/reference/property-object |
| **Gmail** | Label `id` "immutable"; `name` is "Parent/Child" — the slash *is* the nesting; messages reference label ids | Split: id is identity, path lives in the mutable name | Renaming the parent renames every child's *name* (third-party docs); message references are untouched because they hold ids | https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/reference/rest/v1/users.labels ; https://developer.nylas.com/docs/cookbook/email/gmail-labels-api/ |

| **TagSpaces** | Tags as strings in the filename `IMG[vacation alps].jpg` or in a sidecar `.ts/*.json`; tag groups are a library-side organisation | **Yes** — string | Not documented; nothing in the file carries a tag id, so a rename is a per-file rewrite by construction | https://docs.tagspaces.org/tagging/ |

| **Hats Protocol (on-chain)** | Hat id is a uint256 bitmap: top-hat 32 bits + fourteen 16-bit levels; admins derived from the id | **Yes** — hierarchy in the id | `details` string is mutable (`changeHatDetails`, requires the hat be mutable unless a tophat); a hat cannot be re-parented, only whole trees can be linked/relinked under another admin | https://docs.hatsprotocol.xyz/for-developers/hats-protocol-for-developers/hat-ids ; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Hats-Protocol/hats-protocol/main/src/Hats.sol |

The pattern: **every tool with a database put identity in an id and hierarchy in a mutable relation; every tool without one (files, notes, xattrs) put both in a string and pays per-item rewrites at rename.** An immutable ledger cannot do per-item rewrites at all, so the string-identity shape is not merely expensive on EFS — it is the shape whose only repair mechanism is unavailable.

## 6. Rename, merge, split — what breaks, what preserves old references, whether history is kept

| System | Rename/merge mechanism | Old references | History |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Danbooru** | Alias approval runs `TagMover.move!`: per post `remove_tag(old); add_tag(new); save!`, then moves wiki, artist, aliases, implications, saved searches, blacklists, wiki links (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/danbooru/danbooru/master/app/logical/tag_mover.rb). Aliases are **non-transitive** by validator ("We don't want a -> b && b -> c chains"). Implications are applied at **write time** (`PostEdit`: `TagAlias.to_aliased(...)` then `tag_names += TagImplication.tags_implied_by(tag_names)`) **and retroactively** on approval (`update_posts!` re-saves every post matching `A -B`); `descendants_of` is recursive, so implications are transitive. | The old tag record persists with zero posts; searches for it redirect | Every retag creates a post version — full edit history, which is what Sankaku/Danbooru users rely on (`booru-app.md:49-53`) |
| **Hydrus** | Siblings (A→B) and parents (C→P) are **virtual**: "Although the client may present A as B, it will secretly remember A!"; "No information is lost at any point"; siblings transitive, acyclic, one ideal per tag; parents apply after sibling resolution; removing a relationship makes everything revert (https://hydrusnetwork.github.io/hydrus/advanced_siblings.html, …/advanced_parents.html). Per tag service, so two repositories can disagree. | Storage tags untouched | Storage vs display distinction *is* the history |
| **Stack Overflow** | Synonym: new taggings "automatically and silently remapped" to the master; retroactive merge is a moderator function (2010 blog) | Synonym remains as a search alias | The user's original tag is lost at write |
| **Wikidata** | Merge → redirect; KrBot rewrites referring statements within days; redirects are never deleted; double redirects collapsed | Preserved via redirect + `owl:sameAs` | Revision history on both items; undo needs bot cooperation |
| **Wikipedia categories** | CfD → bot edits every member page; old page becomes `{{category redirect}}` | Soft redirect only; members must be moved | Page histories; ~6.4M bot edits |
| **LCSH** | Cancel heading, establish replacements; libraries rewrite 650 strings | Cancelled heading survives (in practice) as a reference; not verified from LC's own record (403) | Seven-year political process; 40+ local forks |
| **Gmail / Notion** | Rename the name on a stable id | Untouched | None needed |
| **macOS / Obsidian / TagSpaces / XMP** | Rewrite every item | Lost | None |
| **Bluesky** | No rename; a labeler negates old `val` and emits a new one; definitions are per-labeler and can be republished | Old labels remain in the stream, retracted | Full signed stream from cursor 0 |
| **EFP** | No rename; `untag`/`tag` ops appended | Ops are append-only | The op log is the history |

Two rules fall out. **(1) Write-time rewriting is only available to systems that own a mutable store** (Danbooru, SO, Wikidata bots, Cydebot, LC). An immutable ledger is structurally Hydrus, not Danbooru: aliases *must* resolve at read time, and the alias graph must be versioned so a query can pin a vocabulary basis — which `booru-app.md:209` already specifies ("aliases and implications expand under an explicit vocabulary version/Lens"). **(2) Implications are the exception:** Danbooru needs *both* write-time expansion and retroactive re-save; on EFS, retroactive rewriting of other attesters' claims is impossible, so an implication added later cannot reach old files unless the index is rebuilt or the reader expands at query time. This directly hits the bitmap design: `indexing-and-state:79` and `:433-435` say "images" vs "image/png" is cheap only if the second posting is written at write time — i.e. implication bits are frozen at write time. Say this plainly in the design: write-time-materialised implications give O(1) queries and stale semantics; read-time implications give correct semantics and no on-chain answer beyond one hop.

| **Farcaster channels** | Cast `parentUrl` = `https://farcaster.xyz/~/channel/<name>` stored on hubs; channel metadata, follows, norms live in the Warpcast client | Not on-chain at all; hubs filter by `parentUrl` | https://docs.farcaster.xyz/learn/what-is-farcaster/channels |
| **Ethereum Follow Protocol** | List ops `version\|opcode\|data`; opcodes 1 add, 2 remove, 3 tag, 4 untag; tag = UTF-8, normalized lowercase, ≤255 bytes, alphanumerics + most emoji; `block`/`mute`/`top8` semantics defined in docs | On-chain: `applyListOp(s)` append raw bytes per slot; `getListOp`, `getListOpCount`, `getAllListOps` return **raw ops only** — no current-state or by-tag read; state is derived off-chain. The uniform finding: **no deployed on-chain system enumerates "everything tagged X" on-chain.** Where tags are strings inside data (EAS, ENS, EFP, Farcaster, Lens, NFT JSON) there is no index; where an on-chain check exists (Hats, POAP, ERC-7496) it is a point read keyed by an id. The EFS bitmap plan (`indexing-and-state:124-169`: bits keyed `(scope, tagId)` over the kind-10 directory ordinal, ≈5k gas per bit ESTIMATED, membership two SLOADs) would be the first on-chain tag index with enumeration and negation-within-a-listing. It is keyed by `tagId` — which means the identity choice in §0 lands inside the index: if the id encodes name+parent, a rename produces a new id and the bitmaps for old and new tag **split**; a REDIRECT record does not merge two bit-columns. Concept-keyed ids keep one column across renames.

## 8. Synthesis — the principles that recur, who supports them, who violated them and paid

**P1. Identity is a concept id; every human string is metadata on it.**
Supporters: SKOS (URI vs literals, S14), Wikidata (Q vs labels/aliases; merge → redirect never deleted), Notion (option id "does not change if the name is changed"), Gmail (immutable label id), Hats (id vs mutable `details`), Lightroom/digiKam catalogs, Bluesky (a `val` is scoped by `src`, never global). Violators and their bill: LCSH (7 years, 40+ local forks, string rewrite of every record), Wikipedia categories (Cydebot, ~6.4M edits), macOS tags (rewrite every xattr, Spotlight-dependent), Obsidian (plugin find/replace), XMP keyword paths (three separators, silent loss in `dc:subject`), Danbooru (per-post retag per alias — survivable only because it owns a mutable DB). EFS today: `fable-handoff:70` puts name and parent in `tagId`; `booru-app.md:128-137` does not. Pick the second for tag concepts. Keep the first for **paths** — a filesystem path must resolve deterministically and the handoff's red team is right that it needs canonical-name validation and permanence (`fable-handoff:71`). They are different objects: a TagConcept is *placed* at a path via a naming slot (`fable-handoff:76` "naming-slot = TAGDEF + one PIN"), exactly as `layered-type-system:731-739` places a Type in a catalog without making the path its identity.

**P2. Hierarchy is a claim over ids, not part of the id — except for paths.**
Supporters: SKOS broader (a triple; a concept in many schemes; not transitive by default), Wikidata P279/P361 (statements, cycle-tracked), Danbooru implications (versioned, transitive, retroactive), Hydrus parents (virtual, reversible), Notion status groups, Gmail (nesting lives in the mutable name, not the id). Violators: Obsidian, XMP paths, Hats (cannot re-parent a hat), ENS subnames. The owner's "/clouds/nimbus shows as Nimbus under the clouds grouper" is two claims on one concept: a label claim (`prefLabel "Nimbus"@en`) and a grouping claim (member of `clouds`). Both are TAG/PIN edges under reserved keys in the tags-maximalist vocabulary (`tags-maximalist.md:184-199` already has a reserved-key table; `broader`, `member-of`, `label`, `alias-of` are rows, not new kinds). Rename = one new label claim; regroup = one new edge; neither touches the files.

**P3. Type the hierarchy relation, or accept that transitive walks are unsafe.**
ISO 25964 BTG/BTP/BTI and Alexiev et al. 2015 show mixed chains produce false inferences; SKOS refused to type `broader` and says so; Wikidata typed it and still fights P31/P279 confusion; Wikipedia distinguishes container/set/topic categories; SKOS Collections and Wikipedia container categories both say **a grouper is not a concept and must not sit in the broader chain.** The handoff's "is X under /pizza = walk `_parents`" (`fable-handoff:70`) is sound for paths (one homogeneous containment relation) and unsound for tag concepts unless the edge key names the relation. Recommend at least three reserved relation keys: `broader` (generic), `partOf`, `memberOf` (grouper), with `memberOf` targets excluded from transitive walks, per SKOS §9.6.4.

**P4. Aliases resolve at read time; implications are the hard case.**
Evidence: the only systems that rewrite at write time own a mutable store (Danbooru, SO, Wikidata bots, Cydebot, LC). Immutable or federated stores resolve at read: Hydrus (siblings virtual, "no information is lost", one ideal, acyclic, transitive), WDQS (`owl:sameAs` over redirects), SKOS (`exactMatch`, explicitly not `owl:sameAs` merging). Bluesky has no aliases at all and does fine because labels are per-labeler behaviours. For EFS: aliases = `sameAs`/`exactMatch` claims read under a pinned vocabulary basis (`booru-app.md:209`), never a rewrite. Implications split: Danbooru shows correct semantics need *retroactive* application, which EFS cannot do to other attesters' claims. So either materialise implied bits at write time (fast, frozen at write, same staleness Danbooru fixes with `update_posts!`) or expand at read time (correct, no on-chain enumeration beyond one hop). The design must choose per query class; `query-and-indexing.md:97-101` (Q3/Q4) is where that ruling belongs.

**P5. Retraction is not denial; history is never rewritten.**
Bluesky `neg` ("does not mean the inverse"); `booru-app.md:157-158` polarity ASSERT|DENY as separate evidence; Danbooru post versions; Hydrus storage-vs-display; Wikidata "never delete redirects"; EFP append-only ops. Violators: Stack Overflow's *silent* remap (the user's own word is lost), macOS/Obsidian renames. EFS already has this by construction (REVOKE ≠ DENY claim); keep DENY as a first-class claim, not a revoke.

**P6. Pluralism = per-source vocabularies + subscription + per-source viewer policy; the combinator depends on the claim's cardinality.**
Bluesky: definitions scoped per labeler; 20 subscriptions; per-labeler user preference; union of causes then most-restrictive by priority; Bluesky's own labeler hard-included. Hydrus: siblings/parents per tag service. Nostr: namespaces, weaker because labeler identity is not the scope. LCSH: single authority → captured by Congress in 2016. The EFS Lens (`fable-handoff:33`, first-attester-wins) is the correct combinator for card-1 bindings and the wrong one for card-N tag claims; `visual-gallery:157-168` and `booru-app.md:164-167` ("Curator policy selects which assertions count … it does not erase the rest") already ask for union-with-provenance. Define the card-N combinator explicitly: union over subscribed principals, per-principal viewer policy (include/warn/hide, as Bluesky), DENY from a subscribed principal wins over ASSERT by priority, not by order.

**P7. Convergence is produced at the write UI, not in the contract.**
Golder & Huberman (stability after ~100 bookmarks; imitation via displayed tags), Halpin (preferential attachment; UI artefacts in tag counts), Suchanek (≈⅓ of applications induced by suggestions), Stack Overflow (creation friction + silent synonym remap). Contract-level vocabulary enforcement appears nowhere in a working system; OSM runs on "any tags you like" plus wiki documentation. EFS should spend its gas on the id and the index, and its UX on autocomplete from the Lens's existing concepts plus a visible cost to mint a new one.

**P8. On-chain queryability requires the tag to be a first-class key at write time, and the key must survive renames.**
Every deployed on-chain tag is either an unindexed string (EAS data, ENS `keywords`, EFP ops, Farcaster `parentUrl`, Lens/NFT JSON) or a point-read id (Hats, POAP, ERC-7496). EFS's bitmap-over-ordinal is the first proposal with on-chain enumeration and in-listing negation, and it is keyed by `tagId`. P1 is therefore not a taste question: a name-bearing `tagId` splits the index column on every rename, and no REDIRECT record re-merges bits. Gas framing (all from the vault): a tag write today is 2,838,264 gas / 94 slots MEASURED (`gas-engineering:30-34`); a fresh slot is 22,100 today and 110,020 under Glamsterdam (`indexing-and-state:37-41`, QUOTED parts, ESTIMATED sum); a concept-id design adds at most two records to *first* use of a new tag (concept + label; the placement PIN exists either way, `tags-maximalist.md:213`), and makes every later rename one record instead of N. Under the Glamsterdam gap (allocate 9.1× a rewrite), the design that avoids new id allocation on rename is the one that stays affordable.

## 9. Direct answers to the owner's two framings

- **"/clouds/nimbus shows as 'Nimbus' but we know it's under the clouds grouper."** SKOS, Wikipedia and Notion all model this the same way: `nimbus` is a concept with a label; `clouds` is either a broader *concept* (things about clouds — a `broader` claim) or a *grouper* (a Collection / container category / status group — a `memberOf` claim), and the two must not be confused because groupers are excluded from transitive reasoning. The path `/clouds/nimbus` is a third thing: a catalog placement, useful for browsing, not identity (`layered-type-system:741-751`). All three can coexist on one concept id.
- **"Booru dynamic tags whose metadata (related tags, parent tag) can be updated."** That is SKOS `related`/`broader` and Danbooru implications/wiki as *mutable claims over a stable concept*, which is exactly `booru-app.md:128-137`. The thing to avoid is Danbooru's *mechanism* for renames (retag every post), which is unavailable on an immutable ledger; Hydrus's virtual siblings/parents are the deployed proof that read-time resolution over a stable tag store works for a booru-class corpus.
